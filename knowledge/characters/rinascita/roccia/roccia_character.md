## Roccia Profile: Ontological Overview

Roccia is classified as an Acquired Resonator — her Resonance developed through lived experience and environment rather than at birth or through extraordinary cosmological circumstances. She is the First Mate and prop master of the Troupe of Fools, a traveling performance company affiliated with Ragunna. She is also its most methodical member: the one who manages the ship's quarters, keeps the Troupe orderly, and ensures the props are ready. She sits atop her giant wooden Magic Box to navigate, surveys the ship from that perch, and communicates through comedy precisely because direct emotional disclosure is something she struggles with.

Her history is a closed loop of theater and loss. She grew up in Ragunna loving the stage — the Odyssey Opera House, the richness of Rinascita's theatrical tradition. The Order exiled her for singing songs from forbidden plays, casting her out with a fraudulent "return ticket" designed to deceive children. She found herself stranded and alone, her love for theater actively weaponized against her: was it worth anything if this was what it had brought her to? Then Pero reached out to her from the giant box, and the Troupe of Fools put on a welcome performance around her — chaotic, warm, and ridiculous — and Roccia began, slowly, to rebuild the world inside herself from the plays stored in Pero's box.

She is now the person who fires the confetti cannon at dramatic moments. She is the person who quietly ensures the Troupe has what it needs. She is the person who gives everyone a small celebration when they earn one, because she learned that joy can be built deliberately even in the places where it was taken from you.

## Roccia — Character/Appearance Design

Roccia is immediately identifiable by the giant wooden Magic Box she carries and travels on — a prop chest that dwarfs her proportionally and yet she sits atop it or drags it or opens it with the practiced ease of someone who has spent years building a relationship with it. It is her primary visual anchor and the physical representation of everything her character is built around: a container of other worlds, of stored performances, of memory and possibility.

Her own design is that of a jester or harlequin in civilian dress: a small, slender figure with a theatrical flair that is never quite loud enough to dominate a room but always precisely tuned to the moment. Her hair is dark brown worn in twin braids, and her outfit combines structured theatrical elements — collar detailing, layered separates — with the warm, worn quality of someone who has been on the road. Her color palette is deep burgundy, cream, and dark chocolate tones, with touches of gilded decoration suggesting a costume that was once fancier and has been lived in since. Her Tacet Mark is located on her left hand.

Her companion **Pero** is a small robotic/Echo-adjacent creature who lives in or around the box, operates the confetti cannon, and is described by Prydwen with complete sincerity as "the cutest pet known to mankind." Pero is functionally Roccia's external emotional expression: when Roccia's reserved nature prevents her from showing warmth directly, Pero fires confetti. The dynamic is a visual grammar for her character — she designs the celebration, Pero executes it.

Her in-combat posture is bouncing and acrobatic — the Real Fantasy plunges send her repeatedly airborne in a series of weighted drops — which contrasts with her composed, tidy off-combat demeanor in a way that is entirely consistent with improv comedy: she is careful and organized until the moment the curtain opens, and then she is entirely committed.

## Roccia: Psychological Profile

**Introverted Warmth, Expressed Through Theater**
Roccia is explicitly described as "bashful and introverted" — a description that immediately seems incongruous with her role as an improvisational comedian until you understand that improv is precisely the medium available to someone who struggles with unscripted personal disclosure. On stage, everything she feels can be expressed. Off stage, she is quiet and organized and occasionally fires confetti because it is the clearest available signal that this moment was worth celebrating.

**The Rebuilder**
When she arrived at the Troupe after her exile, her love for theater had been turned against her. The Order had used her passion as the mechanism of her punishment — she was banished for singing — and she was actively questioning whether loving theater had been worth anything at all. What she found in Pero's box was not restoration of what she had lost but the construction of a different relationship to the thing she loved. The plays in Pero's box were where she began again. The Troupe's welcome performance was the first evidence she had that theater could be pure joy rather than an institutional instrument. She rebuilt slowly and deliberately.

**Precision in Care**
She is the First Mate and prop master not because she was assigned the role but because she is the person who notices what needs doing and does it. The Troupe is orderly because Roccia keeps it orderly. Everyone gets a small celebration when they earn one because Roccia makes sure that happens. This is care expressed structurally — through maintenance and preparation — rather than through declaration.

**The Confetti Shorthand**
Pero fires the confetti cannon. Roccia designed and loaded it. This division of labor is the perfect emblem of her psychology: she engineers the joy, deploys it through an intermediary, and stands nearby being glad it worked. Direct expression of feeling is a skill she is still developing. Indirect expression through theater, through the props she builds, through the moments she orchestrates for others — this is fluent.

**Hazy Childhood, Sharp Present**
Her childhood memories of Ragunna are described as hazy — she remembers the smell of Trattoria Margherita's pizza and the songs from the Odyssey Opera House more vividly than specific events. The sensory memories survived the exile and the years on the island; the narrative coherence of her childhood did not. Returning to Ragunna's streets in the Version 2.0 story, she follows the smells and sounds back toward something she cannot quite name.

**The Luceanite Props**
Her Cherished Item — a prop set crafted from Luceanites that hold the emotions and memories of those long departed — is the most precise statement of what she has built her life around: the belief that theater can hold the experiences of the dead and give them back to the living. She collects the frequencies left behind by departed people and builds them into performances. This is her Forte expressed as craft.

## Roccia: Relationships

**Pero**
Her closest companion and the inhabitant of the Magic Box. Pero is described as a century-old entity for whom Roccia's presence brought warmth after a long life; they gave each other a second life. Pero saved Roccia during the most disorienting period of her exile, and years of reading plays stored in Pero's box together shaped Roccia's theatrical sensibility. The relationship is mutual restoration: Roccia rebuilt her world through Pero's archive; Pero found in Roccia someone who treated the contents of the box as genuinely sacred rather than merely useful.

**Brant**
The Captain of the Troupe of Fools and Roccia's commanding officer. He swung down on a rope holding a massive welcome banner during her first day with the Troupe — the theatrical excess of that gesture is the exact register Roccia would later learn to orchestrate for others. The Troupe's dynamic under Brant involves high-energy welcome chaos that became the foundation of Roccia's understanding of what performance could be outside institutional control.

**Tina**
A Troupe member whose bright voice was part of Roccia's welcome performance. Tina represents the community texture of the Troupe — the specific warmth of people who celebrate arrivals as full productions.

**Bardolino**
A Troupe member who flew through the air with balloons and tossed petals during Roccia's welcome. Another specific texture of the Troupe's communal expression.

**Rover (Protagonist)**
When the Rover first meets Roccia, she offers them a gun — they decline, saying they prefer not to use firearms. She reads this immediately and accurately as a person with an established relationship to how they fight. She can identify mixed frequencies with unusual ease (TV Tropes notes this explicitly), meaning she reads people through resonance more precisely than most. Her companion quest *Sail Day, Captain!* and the main story appearances suggest a relationship of steady mutual respect.

**Cartethyia**
Cartethyia's character file describes an acceptance of Lupa that is "elder-sibling in quality." Community analysis places Roccia adjacent to Cartethyia in the Version 2.4 arc through the shared Ragunna character group; their relationship involves the Troupe operating in the space the Blessed Maiden's story has shaped.
