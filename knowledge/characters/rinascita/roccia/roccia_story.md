## Roccia Official Introduction

*Roccia is a gifted improvisational comedian and the First Mate of the Fool's Troupe, known for her mastery of stage props. With a tranquil, composed disposition, she navigates the ship's quarters, perched atop her giant box, meticulously ensuring the Troupe remains orderly and tidy.*

Assistant, prop master, and improv comedian, Roccia is always there to make sure the Troupe of Fools is at the ready. The Magic Box she carries appears to hold the world — or perhaps she had recreated a world inside the box.

## Roccia Personality

Roccia is one of the few characters in Wuthering Waves whose defining characteristic is not her power or her history but her method: she is someone who discovered that joy can be built deliberately, even in the places where it was taken from you. The discovery was not theoretical. She arrived at it through the specific experience of having theater — the thing she loved — used against her, and then finding, in the Troupe of Fools, that theater could also be the mechanism of reconstruction.

She is quiet and reserved in a way that reads as composure and is partially composure and partially the fact that she communicates emotional warmth most fluently when she has a prop in hand. She fires confetti at moments of celebration not because she lacks the words but because confetti is more honest than words about how happy she is. She has gotten better at words. She has not stopped using confetti.

Her Forte lets her read mixed resonance frequencies with unusual precision — she perceives the emotional residue left behind in objects, places, and Luceanites. This is why she collects Luceanites from the Strand: each one holds the emotions and memories of someone who has departed, and she builds those frequencies into props that she brings onto the stage. Her belief is that theater performed with these props lets the original keepers of those emotions shine again. The stage, for Roccia, is not a place of pretense. It is the place where the true things in objects finally get to speak.

Her Convene quote — *"All the world's a stage, and we are mere players"* — references Shakespeare's *As You Like It*. From a character who was exiled for singing forbidden plays and who rebuilt her world through the plays stored in her companion's box, the reference is neither casual nor purely theatrical. It is a position statement: she has accepted that the world is the stage and she is one of its players, and she has decided that acceptance is not resignation but the beginning of craft.

## Roccia Forte Examination Report

Roccia's Forte classification is **Acquired Resonator**. Her Resonance did not manifest at birth or through cosmological circumstance but through the accumulated experience of exile, loss, and the slow reconstruction of her world through theatrical performance and the frequencies she perceives in Luceanites and stored memories.

Her spectral waveform is stable with no Overclocking risk noted in available records. The nature of her Forte manifests primarily as an ability to identify and work with mixed resonance frequencies — she can distinguish emotional residue in objects and Luceanites that most Resonators would read as ambient noise. This ability is deployed practically: she uses it to craft props from Luceanites, embedding the felt histories of departed people into stage materials.

The examiner recorded: *"She described them not as data but as a feeling — like listening to a performance that ended before you arrived but left the warmth of the audience still in the seats."* The description is noted as scientifically non-quantifiable and emotionally precise.

## Roccia Character Story I: "The Songs from Forbidden Plays"

*When she opened her eyes, she found herself face-to-face with a peculiar box and a long arm reaching out to her — a hand offering her a second chance at life.*

Roccia grew up in Ragunna loving the theater. The Odyssey Opera House. The plays that circulated through the city's culture. The songs that everyone knew and the ones that were technically not supposed to be performed anymore because the Order had decided they were not aligned with approved expression. She sang them anyway, the way children sing things they love without fully accounting for institutional consequence.

The Pilgrim's Sail came with what it described as a return ticket. She was a child. She believed it. The exile was designed to look like something else, and she was stranded on an island far from Ragunna with nothing but the music she had been punished for loving and the question of whether it had been worth anything at all.

She lay there with that question. And then the box reached out to her.

*Out came a beautifully crafted mask, painted with intricate patterns and adorned with pearls and shells. "Pero! Pero!"* The box reached toward her again. Tina's bright voice rang out. Bardolino flew through the air with balloons and tossed petals. Brant swung down on a rope holding a massive welcome banner. Pero fired the confetti cannon. This was her introduction to the Troupe of Fools: a performance, entirely for her, that was nothing but joy. No Order tenets. No approved content. Just the full chaotic exuberance of people who had decided that theater was for celebrating the people in the audience.

She had never seen theater used for that. She had not known it could be.

## Roccia Character Story II: "The World Inside the Box"

*For years, Roccia and Pero explored every inch of the Troupe's home base, every stretch of the island shore. They collected countless Luceanites from the Strand and watched thousands of sunrises.*

The island was confined. The Troupe had been confined there for longer than Roccia had been with them — long enough that the Echo ship had starred as a lifeboat, a windmill giant's boot, and the eye of a sea monster across various productions. The island was not large. What was inside Pero's box was.

Roccia discovered this gradually. The plays stored in Pero's archive were not a collection in the library sense — they were an inhabited space. Reading them with Pero meant encountering the emotional residue of the people who had written them, performed them, wept and laughed at them. Her Forte, which can read the frequencies left in objects, found in the plays an inexhaustible source of material. She was not merely reading scripts. She was receiving transmissions.

She began to craft from what she found. The props she built were Luceanite-embedded — made from stones collected on the Strand that held the emotions and memories of the departed, the frequencies of people who had left an impression on the world and then left the world. She believed, and still believes, that staging a performance with these props allows the original keepers of those frequencies to shine again through the material. The stage holds them one more time.

The Troupe was her world inside a confined island. Pero's box was the world inside the Troupe. The Luceanites were the worlds inside the world inside the world. She has been building her world at every nested level since the day Pero's arm reached out to her, and she has never found the bottom of it.

## Roccia Character Story III: "Homeward Wind"

*Childhood memories were hazy for Roccia, but as she followed Brant and the crew into Ragunna's streets, the familiar smell of Trattoria Margherita's fresh-baked pizza and the songs spilling from the Odyssey Opera House stirred something deep within her, just like it did everyone else.*

When the Troupe finally received the signal that let them leave the island — the Laureate's music, the homeward wind — Roccia did not know quite what she was returning to. Her childhood memories of Ragunna were hazy in the narrative sense: she could not reconstruct the chronology, the specific events, the faces. What she could reconstruct was sensory. The smell of the trattoria. The particular texture of the opera house songs leaking into the street. These had survived everything.

She walked into Ragunna behind Brant and the others and discovered, not for the first time, that the place she remembered was not the place that existed. The Ragunna she had loved — alive with plays, flowers, and freedom — had become the place the Order had made it: curated, controlled, with only the approved songs playing and only the approved stories told. The plays she had been exiled for were still forbidden. The Order had not relaxed.

This is the specific context for her presence in the Version 2.0 main story. The Troupe of Fools arrives in Ragunna as a performance company whose entire identity is the deliberate staging of joy outside institutional permission. Their presence in the city is a form of argument. Every performance is an unauthorized expression of theater as it was before it became a tool of the Order. Roccia, who knows precisely what it cost to sing the wrong song in this city, is the one who makes sure the props are ready.

She does not make speeches about it. She fires confetti at the right moments.

## Roccia: Cherished Items

**Luceanite Prop Set**
A unique prop set crafted by Roccia from Luceanites collected on the island Strand. Luceanites hold the emotions and memories of those long departed — residual frequencies left in matter by people who felt things strongly enough that the feeling persisted past their lives. Roccia's Forte lets her identify these frequencies precisely, and she builds them into stage materials so that the performances they appear in are partly inhabited by the people whose feelings they carry. *When brought onto the stage, these lingering feelings glimmer within each line and monologue, casting a light that, as Roccia believes, allows their original keepers to shine once more.* She does not know if this is metaphysically true. She acts on it as though it is, which may amount to the same thing.

**Pero's Box (Symbolic — the archive)**
The giant wooden Magic Box is technically Pero's, not Roccia's, but the relationship between Roccia and the box is indistinguishable from ownership in the relevant sense: she has read everything in it, built her world from its contents, and carries it everywhere. Its physical presence in her kit as the object she bounces off during Real Fantasy, and its functional presence as the source of her Inherent Skill's Magic Box utility transfer, makes it the most accurate representation of her character in combat: the thing she returns to, again and again, is the box.

## Roccia: Favorite/Disliked Food

**Favorite:** The smell of fresh-baked pizza from Trattoria Margherita in Ragunna — this is the sensory memory she retained most vividly through her years of exile. Whether this constitutes a food preference or a memory preference is a question the text does not settle. She returns to Ragunna and it is one of the first things she recognizes.

**Disliked:** Foods eaten under duress or as part of institutional hospitality — the kind consumed when you are being processed through a system that does not have your wellbeing as its primary interest. The Pilgrim's Sail probably included meals. She does not think about those meals.

## Roccia: Ideals

**Passion — Theater as Homebuilding**
Roccia did not inherit a positive relationship to theater. She had one, it was weaponized against her, she questioned whether it was worth anything, and she rebuilt it from a different foundation — the foundation Pero's box and the Troupe's chaotic welcome performance provided. The theater she practices now is not the theater of the Order's approved scripts. It is the theater of *pure joy*, as she explicitly names it, and of the frequencies in Luceanites, and of small celebrations for moments that deserve one. This is not just a passion. It is a comprehensive life philosophy arrived at through a specific loss.

**Resonance — The Small Celebrations**
She adds confetti at cheerful, dramatic moments — when a troupe member says something quick-witted, when something deserves its own small spectacle. This is described as deliberate: *each deserving its own small celebration.* She has made the decision that these moments are worth marking. This is the most direct expression of what she learned from the Troupe's welcome — that joy can be performed for someone, that the performance of joy is itself a form of care.

**Gratitude — Pero**
The relationship with Pero is described as mutually restorative: Roccia rebuilt her world through Pero's archive; Pero found warmth after a century of existence through Roccia's presence. They gave each other a second life. Roccia's gratitude toward Pero is not something she articulates in speeches. It is structural: she carries the box everywhere, she fires the confetti through Pero's mechanism, she makes sure Pero is part of every performance. The gratitude is expressed through continued practice.

**Acceptance — "All the World's a Stage"**
The Shakespeare quote she uses to introduce herself in her Convene is the whole of her orientation in one sentence. She has accepted that she is a player in a world-as-stage — that the Order, the exile, the island, the homecoming, the forbidden songs, the Luceanites, the confetti, the three Real Fantasy bounces — all of it is the performance she is currently in, and her job is to know her role and play it with everything she has. This is not passivity. Players who know the stage can also rewrite the scene.

## Roccia: Narrative Chronicle

- **Early childhood, Ragunna:** Born January 31, Ragunna, Rinascita. Grows up loving the theatrical culture of Ragunna — the Odyssey Opera House, the plays, the music. Sings songs from plays the Order has classified as forbidden.
- **Exile:** The Order issues a Pilgrim's Sail against Roccia for singing songs from forbidden plays. She is sent away with what is presented as a return ticket — a deception designed for children. She is stranded on an island far from Ragunna.
- **Finding the Troupe:** In the lowest point of her exile, a peculiar box reaches out to her — Pero extends an arm. The Troupe of Fools performs an elaborate welcoming ceremony: Tina, Bardolino, Brant, and Pero all participate. Roccia receives her first confetti, her first performance as an audience of one, her first experience of theater as pure joy.
- **The island years:** Roccia and Pero spend years on the island and on the Troupe's home base. She explores the shores, collects Luceanites from the Strand, and reads through the plays stored in Pero's box. Her Forte develops its capacity to identify mixed frequencies; she begins crafting props from Luceanite material. She becomes the Troupe's First Mate and prop master.
- **The homeward wind:** When the Laureate's music finally signals the permission to return, the Troupe feels the homeward pull. Childhood memories are hazy for Roccia, but the smells and sounds of Ragunna's streets stir something recognizable as she returns.
- **Version 2.0 Prologue and Acts — Ragunna arc:** The Troupe of Fools arrives in Ragunna. Roccia is present throughout the main story arc (Chapter II Prologue, Act II: *Veils Off in Sun or Shadow*, Act III: *What Yesterday Wept, Today Doth Sing*). The Rover first meets Roccia in Ragunna; she offers them a gun, they decline. Her Forte's precise frequency-reading allows her to identify mixed resonance patterns during the arc's investigation sequences.
- **Version 2.0 Phase 2 — Playable Release (January 22, 2025):** Roccia becomes playable. The Imagination/Beyond Imagination/Real Fantasy kit encodes her theatrical identity: resources built by building momentum, a peak performance window of three aerial bounces, and a Liberation that buffs every member of the team based on how precisely she has tuned her own statistics — because a prop master who knows the exact capacity of her instruments can give everyone what they need.
- **Companion Quest — Sail Day, Captain!:** Roccia's companion story set during or after the Version 2.0 arc, exploring her history with the Troupe and her relationship with Pero in more detail.
- **Version 2.0 Act X and XI:** Roccia appears in later Chapter II acts (*The Bygone Shall Always Return*, *Dawn Breaks on Dark Tides*), suggesting her arc continues through the conclusion of the Ragunna story.
