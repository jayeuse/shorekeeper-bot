# Phoebe Story Documentation
<!-- Sources: https://genshin-builds.com/en/wuthering-waves/characters/phoebe/info, https://wutheringwaves.fandom.com/wiki/Phoebe/Backstory, https://wutheringwaves.fandom.com/wiki/Phoebe -->

## Phoebe Official Introduction

*Phoebe, an Acolyte of the Order of the Deep, always carries herself with warmth and grace. Devout and steadfast in her adherence to the Order's doctrines, she embodies exceptional self-discipline. Yet beneath her composed exterior burns a vibrant spirit, alight with heartfelt joy for all she holds dear.*

*A young woman of quiet devotion and compassionate, nurturing nature, she upholds the teachings of her faith with unwavering diligence. Raised her entire life as a devout Acolyte, she extends her heartfelt kindness to everyone she meets with her good graces and unquestionable reverence to Sentinel Imperator.*

## Phoebe Personality

Phoebe is the product of an extraordinary number of losses met with an extraordinary quality of response. She lost her parents, lost the family's possessions, lost her father's locket to the sea, nearly lost her own life, and was turned away by every relative who might have given her a home. The orphanage was her second-best outcome, and she experienced it as a gift. This is not naivety — it is a child who genuinely processed the alternative and chose gratitude for what remained, and an adult who has kept that orientation active through discipline rather than through forgetting the alternatives.

Her defining quality is that her care for others is practical and immediately responsive. She does not theorize about compassion — she hands you the ice cream before you have finished crying. She files the incident report before the other Acolytes arrive at the restaurant. She untangles the kite string from Brenno before she has even thought of what this Echo's existence will mean to her next decade. The action comes first; the articulation follows if it comes at all.

Her faith is both genuine and specific. She does not believe in the Order of the Deep as an organization with infallible authority. She believes in the Divinity. These two things have been synonymous in her experience — the Order fed her, sheltered her, gave her a name for her light — but they are not the same, and the moment she begins to notice the gap between them (Story IV, watching Rover's Laurel crowning; the shadow from the Atrium of Reflections) she is precise enough not to collapse the distinction. Her doubt is institutional, not theological. She does not doubt the Sentinel; she begins to doubt whether the Order's current interpretation of the Sentinel is always right.

The detail in her Thoughts III voiceline — "This must be one of the Sentinel's trials, a test to see if I can fulfill my duties as an Acolyte while holding onto such ties... It's just, every time I see them, I want to hug them tightly... No, no, no, I must resist!" — is the most direct portrait of her inner life. She has rationalized the rule, found a framework that makes it bearable, and is still losing the fight with herself in real time. The theological rationalization is sincere; so is the struggle. She experiences both without collapsing either.

Her Ideals voiceline, trailing off when asked what she hopes for herself, is the most significant gap in her self-knowledge. Not a repressed desire but an underdeveloped one: she has been hoping outward for so long that the inward direction is genuinely unfamiliar. The Rinascita storyline and her companion story work to address this — the relationship with Rover being, among other things, the first context in which someone asks Phoebe what she wants and waits for the real answer.

A light so comforting it conveys the very essence of hope in the darkest of times. As written in the Tenets of the Deep, such light must have always existed in her heart, a sign of her relentless devotion.

*Acolyte Phoebe has always strictly adhered to the teachings of "silence" and "self-restraint," making her stability unmatched among her peers. Observations show that Acolyte Phoebe's Resonance ability produces a stable frequency, strongly affecting Echoes and Remnant Creatures. *Latest Notes: A brief sawtooth fluctuation was detected in Acolyte Phoebe's regular waveform. No abnormalities have been noted at this time, but continued monitoring is advised, with increased attention if necessary.*

**Analysis:** The Forte report reveals the institutional tension at the core of Phoebe's story before the character stories begin. The orphanage recognizes her light as a sign of faith; the Order recognizes her faith as the source of her power; and then the Order itself moves to separate her from the Echoes that demonstrate that faith in its most direct form. The "latest notes" sawtooth fluctuation is a small but significant detail — her waveform has been stable throughout her entire recorded history, making any deviation worth watching. It is the first mechanical indication that something is changing.

## Phoebe Character Story I: "A Day in the Life of an Acolyte"

*On an ordinary afternoon, the wide streets of Ragunna bustled with people, more lively than in previous years. The long-anticipated Carnevale was set to return.*

*What started as a small disagreement over taste at Trattoria Margherita soon escalated into a heated debate. Perhaps it was the weariness of restraint, or perhaps the sleepy afternoon simply needed a bit of excitement. More and more people gathered, joining the argument. The debate grew fiercer and, in the end, exploded into a full-blown fistfight.*

*For a moment, the restaurant became a stage. Pizzas and desserts flew through the air, seafood leaped from plates and landed on the faces of passersby, and onlookers scrambled to hide, though many couldn't help but peek back to watch the chaos unfold.*

*"Everyone! Oh, don't do that! You'll shatter the plates!" The owner cried out, her tail puffed up as she jumped up and down, waving a pizza paddle.*

*Just then, a small figure darted from the crowd. "Please, calm down!"*

*With a staff in hand, the little figure traced a wide arc through the air. A golden bird descended from the sky, seemingly carrying the weight of the heavens. It barely touched the two enraged men on their foreheads, like a dragonfly skimming the surface of a pond.*

*The two men, who had been at each other's throats just moments ago, instantly calmed and fell face-first onto the table with smiles, snoring softly.*

*"Don't worry, they've just fallen asleep," said the petite blonde Acolyte as she tucked away her staff. "Disturbing public order is a violation of Order regulations. Those who disturb the peace must face appropriate punishment."*

*"Temperance is a virtue granted to us by the Sentinel. It makes both food and Nectarwine all the more delicious... I hope this little incident hasn't ruined anyone's enjoyment of their time here in Ragunna."*

*By the time more Acolytes arrived with La Guardia in tow, the commotion had already ended.*

*"Yes, yes, I saw it with my own eyes! She knocked out those two big guys with just one tap of her staff! Then, out of nowhere, a huge Plushie Echo appeared and carried them off!"*

*The lead Acolyte pulled out his Terminal to file a report, only to find that one had already been sent to the Order, detailing the incident and the whereabouts of the two who had been taken away. "So... does that mean it's all taken care of?"*

*"Of course. Leave it to Acolyte Phoebe. She knows how to handle these things better than anyone."*

**Analysis:** The first story establishes Phoebe's competence before establishing her softness. The golden bird Echo, the instant calm, the pre-filed incident report — all of these are the actions of someone extraordinarily capable, not someone sweet and slightly ineffectual. The story's specific pleasure is the gap between expectation (small, gentle Acolyte = uncertain) and reality (the situation is entirely resolved before anyone else even arrives). The structure mirrors Phoebe herself: the warmth is real, but it coexists with precision that most people underestimate.

## Phoebe Character Story II: "Home"

*"Dear Mr. Claremont, [...] Our business partner, the Marinos, were attacked by TDs during their journey to the New Federation. Unfortunately, they did not survive. [...] You must remember Phoebe. The poor girl has lost her parents. She has nowhere to go [...] I pray you might consider offering her a home."*

*I folded the letter carefully, checked the address once more, and knocked again. The repeated knocking had already drawn curious glances from the neighbors, but there was no response from within. The little girl stood quietly behind me, her head bowed, as if accustomed to such situations.*

*"They went on a holiday a while back," a passing neighbor mentioned casually. "Might not be back for quite a while." I tightened my grip on Phoebe's hand, leading her away.*

*Ragunna City was large, yet finding a home here was no easy task. I had lost count of how many doors I had knocked on, and how many times I had been turned away.*

*"We're deeply sorry... You have our sympathy for the girl's misfortune, but you understand adopting a child is more than just adding another plate to the table..."*

*"...I don't recall receiving any letter of this kind. Besides, didn't she already find a home at the orphanage?"*

*"...I've heard that the Marinos still haven't paid off their debts. We'd rather not have creditors knocking on our door."*

*"Don't be sad, Sister Isabella."*

*An ice cream was suddenly held out before me. A group of little Echoes had appeared around me unnoticed, each holding out ice creams and sweets. They were crowded around me, or rather, around Phoebe.*

*"I know the grown-ups are busy, just like Mother and Father were. They always came home so late."*

*I stared blankly at the melting ice cream, caught off guard by the fact that this tiny child was offering comfort to me.*

*"It's okay, I can... live with everyone else. I'm happy being with everyone."*

*When I remained silent for a long while, the young girl looked up at me and gave me a quiet, almost soothing smile. My heart trembled.*

*"Everyone will stay together, right? Sister Isabella? The Acolytes tell us the Sentinel is always with us, keeping us together so we will never be apart."*

*She recited the words with such certainty. In the end, I could only swallow the lump in my throat, hold back my tears, and pull her close.*

*"All right... then let's go back. Let's go back home."*

**Analysis:** Narrated from Sister Isabella's perspective, this is the story's emotional core. Phoebe is not the subject but the agent: she transforms the scene from an adult's failed mission into something she reframes as complete. "I'm happy being with everyone" is both a gift to Isabella (freeing her from guilt) and a genuine statement — Phoebe is old enough to understand that she was rejected, and young enough to find the orphanage genuinely sufficient, and the coexistence of those two truths is what makes her response to Isabella's tears so precise: she knows exactly what kind of comfort is needed and supplies it without ceremony. The Echoes arriving with ice cream is the story's most specific detail — they came because Phoebe was sad, and their coming made her less sad, which is the whole dynamic of that relationship in one image.

## Phoebe Character Story III: "Countless Nights"

*The storm in the dead of night seemed as if it would drain every last drop of water in Ragunna. Lightning flashed across the sky, casting webbed shadows upon a young girl curled up in her blanket.*

*Phoebe couldn't fall asleep. Every time she closed her eyes, she saw the churning, furious sea.*

*On nights like this, her father used to slip a locket with the Sentinel's portrait under her pillow, telling her that the Divinity would guide her through the storms in her dreams. Her mother would place a bouquet of daisies by her bedside and tell her bedtime stories.*

*But now, the Acolytes would simply pat her head and tell her that she was like any other child. Every Rinascitan had to endure suffering before they could receive the Sentinel's forgiveness in the end.*

*Phoebe tried to remember the lullaby her mother used to sing, willing its melody to echo in her mind to drown out the howling storm outside. But still, the relentless thunder and rain dragged her back to that dark night.*

*She had snuck aboard a merchant ship, peering through the porthole of the cargo hold to watch the distant horizon. When her parents returned, their sails would appear from that direction. She didn't want to wait for them anymore. This time, she was going to be brave and find them herself.*

*But the sea wasn't as gentle as her mother described in her stories. She began to hear hurried footsteps on the deck, the noise growing frantic until screams pierced the air as the ship was tossed violently in the storm's grip. The vessel began to sink.*

*Phoebe cried out for her parents, but the raging waves seemed to whisper that they would never return.*

*The icy seawater engulfed her, and in her fading vision, she watched her father's locket disappear into the dark depths of the sea.*

*When she opened her eyes again, she was lying on the shore, soaked to the bone. A Tacet Mark shone on her leg. The Acolytes who found her said no one could survive such a perilous sea, let alone a little girl. They believed it was a blessing from the Sentinel.*

*But Phoebe faintly remembered that something had saved her. A presence so gentle, like a soft breeze, had lifted her to the surface and laid her gently on the shore, telling her:*

*Don't be afraid. I'm here.*

*Something pulled Phoebe from her dream. She felt the bed shift slightly, as though something soft and plush had nestled up beside her. It carried the dampness of the rain and the scent of daisies. Then Phoebe saw a pair of button eyes and soft, floppy ears — it was the stray Echo she had met in Whisperwind Haven earlier that day.*

*Don't be sad, Phoebe.*

*The Plushie's body felt warm and soft. In the haze of sleep, Phoebe felt a faint, familiar presence. It felt like the wind.*

*She buried her face into the Plushie's soft fur and slowly drifted back into a peaceful slumber.*

**Analysis:** The third story reveals the origin of Phoebe's Tacet Mark: a traumatic awakening in a shipwreck she entered voluntarily, trying to find parents who were already dead. The detail of the locket disappearing into the sea before her eyes is devastating in its specificity — it will be recovered by friends later (Cherished Item: "Frozen In Time"), but in the moment of drowning it is the last tangible connection to her parents that she watches go. The presence that saves her — "Don't be afraid. I'm here," like a soft breeze — is ambiguous in its nature. The Acolytes credit the Sentinel. Phoebe's faint memory suggests something more specific. The Plushie Echo Brenno at the end mirrors the rescue: both arrivals are unannounced, both carry warmth in the dark, and both say something to the effect of "I'm here." The story quietly suggests that Phoebe has been saved by the presence of Echoes twice.

## Phoebe Character Story IV: "But Morning Came"

*As Phoebe woke up, she felt a lingering dampness at the corner of her eye. "By the Sentinel's grace, another day to rise and shine!"*

*She leaped out of bed and quickly smoothed the bed sheets. The orphanage was hosting a small celebration, and she had joined her senior Acolyte to help with the preparations. It would be her first time back since becoming an Acolyte Initiate.*

*She set out the tea and snacks and watched the children rush excitedly into their caregivers' arms to receive their carefully wrapped presents. Touched by this warm scene, Phoebe couldn't help but smile.*

*She remembered how her father would always buy her a present during the first stop of his business trips. How her mother would always come up with a story about the present. Her favorite gift had been a pink rabbit stuffed toy. In the story, she was an elegant musician.*

*As the darker parts of her past faded away, Phoebe found herself remembering only the bright, happy moments.*

*A little Echo tugged on her sleeve, proudly showing her a big balloon it had just made. Instinctively, she reached out to pat its head.*

*"Acolyte Initiate Phoebe!" Her senior's stern voice made Phoebe flinch. She quickly withdrew her hand and watched the Echo shuffle off with a hint of sadness.*

*"By the Sentinel's grace, be mindful of your position."*

*...She still hadn't fully adjusted to her new role. As an Acolyte, she couldn't have close attachments to the Echoes.*

*The Sentinel had led her to kind people, given her food to eat and provided a roof over her head. With these gifts came new responsibilities. As an Acolyte, it was her duty to repay such kindness. However, as Phoebe, she still had her own time to meet with friends.*

*[Later:] Phoebe sat on a bench, closing her eyes and letting the soft sea breeze wash over her. Her small figure appeared lonely against the setting sun. Her senior came looking for her, but noticing her shallow breathing realized that she had already fallen asleep.*

*As the girl slept soundly on the bench, quiet little Echoes crept up from the sides and huddled close to her side.*

*The older Acolyte sighed softly, turning away to leave her be.*

*"This time... I'll pretend I didn't see it."*

**Analysis:** The fourth story is the most institutionally precise of the five: it shows exactly how Phoebe navigates the gap between her Acolyte role and her attachment to Echoes, and it shows what the most sympathetic institutional response to this tension looks like. The senior Acolyte pretending not to see is both a small kindness and a small tragedy — the rule remains; it is simply suspended for this one moment because the person enforcing it has enough human recognition to see what it costs. The earlier scene of Phoebe withdrawing her hand from the little Echo is the same tension in its more painful form: the Echo shuffles away with "a hint of sadness," and Phoebe cannot explain, cannot apologize, cannot do anything except maintain the rule and live with the weight of it. The story ends on the Echoes huddling around her in sleep — doing what they have always done: showing up when she needs them and staying without asking for permission.

## Phoebe Character Story V: "Within Distant Brilliance"

*Phoebe still had faint memories of the last Carnevale she had attended. Sitting on her father's shoulders, she watched Wingrays soar overhead as bright streamers and petals fell like rain. Those childhood memories were too hazy to hold onto. Now, whenever she thought of the Carnevale, all that came to mind was a soft glow and distant echoes.*

*Yet she still remembered the expressions on her parents' faces. Their laughter had come from the heart. Memories that once caused her pain now glimmered deep within her.*

*Scenes that had once lived only in her dreams now unfolded before her eyes.*

*She stood among the crowd, watching the dark-haired hero bathe in light as a golden wreath slowly descended, settling atop their head.*

*Her heart skipped a beat. Amidst the cheers, her gaze uncontrollably followed the golden light wherever it went.*

*"It's the Laureate!"*

*"Praise Imperator!"*

*"The Sentinel has sent us a miracle!"*

*Her vision blurred amid the tearful cries of joy.*

*Who wouldn't be moved by such a dazzling sight? Even the Divinity had bestowed a miracle for such a moment. At that instant, Phoebe couldn't help but wonder: how could the Sentinel be angered by the people celebrating the Carnevale? Surely, the Sentinel loved the Carnevale and the joy of the people.*

*The faint shadow that had loomed over her heart since returning from the Atrium of Reflections resurfaced, only to be momentarily washed away by the singing and laughter.*

*But this time, Phoebe clung to that fleeting trace of doubt. The things she had noticed, but had pushed aside. The questions that had risen, but which she had instinctively avoided confronting.*

*Phoebe blinked hard, wiping away the tears at the corners of her eyes. She turned her gaze once again toward the figure surrounded by the crowd.*

*She had an inkling that they might hold the answers to clear her confusion.*

*And one day, she would seek out the truth behind that doubt.*

**Analysis:** The final story is where Phoebe's arc opens rather than closes. The Carnevale scene is the synthesis: her parents' laughter at the Carnevale (recovered from grief), the crowd's joy (which feels consistent with everything she actually understands the Sentinel to value), and the "faint shadow" from the Atrium of Reflections (the institutional doubt she has been suppressing). The significance is in the verb she uses: "this time, Phoebe *clung* to that fleeting trace of doubt." Previous doubts were pushed aside; this one, she decides to keep. It is the first act of intellectual self-assertion she has permitted herself — not against the Sentinel, but on behalf of her own honest observations — and it is made possible by watching Rover crowned in golden light, which in her mind connects to the presence that saved her in the shipwreck, to the light she carries, to everything the Sentinel has ever meant to her in a form she could trust. Rover becomes, in this moment, the person she will one day approach for answers.

## Phoebe: Cherished Items

**Frozen In Time**
A locket Phoebe carries with her, repurposed from an old pocket watch. Inside is a family portrait. It was lost to the depths of the sea during the shipwreck in which her parents died — the last thing she watched disappear as she was drowning. But thanks to the tireless search of her kind friends (strongly implied to be Echoes), it was eventually returned to her. "In the fleeting waves of time... in the beautiful spray of the sea, and in the light, there are words I want to tell you, over and over again." The locket is a physical fulfillment of the miracle that the Acolytes promised was coming, delivered not by institutional intervention but by the friends the institution told her not to get too close to.

**"Envoy"**
A handcrafted souvenir from a land across the sea — the only item Phoebe kept when her family's possessions were sold off to settle the debts after her parents' deaths. In the bedtime stories her mother told that she once believed would never end, there was a bird with blue wings, the envoy of the Divinity. It had the power to cross through storms and bring happiness to the little girl who endured loneliness each night as she drifted to sleep. This item is a material remnant of her mother's storytelling — a private faith older than the Order's formal doctrine, which may be the more foundational of the two.

**"A Vision Far Away"**
An old, faded edition of the Codex of the Deep, handbound by a loving and devout mother. A proper, dutiful Acolyte should be well-versed in the contents of the Codex, holding the Sentinel's guidance close to heart. Phoebe always adhered to the teachings in her heart, though — if truth be told — she preferred the color of the sky to that of the sea. The inscription from her mother reads: "The Codex says that suffering brings us closer to the Divinity. But I still hope for a peaceful and happy life for you, my child." This annotation is the most important text in Phoebe's possession: her mother's quiet, private disagreement with official doctrine — that the official teaching (suffering as spiritual necessity) is real, but love overrides the desired outcome — is identical to the tension Phoebe herself carries as an Acolyte. She inherited the faith and the doubt simultaneously.

## Phoebe: Favorite/Disliked Food

**Favorite:** Pizza — specifically Trattoria Margherita's pizza, which Phoebe references with visible enthusiasm (and visible restraint). Her Favorite Food voiceline reveals "Pizza Day" — the only day of the month she was allowed pizza at the orphanage, a monthly occasion she still thinks about with longing. "The best pizza is one that's shared with friends" is her way of locating the food's meaning correctly: it is not just the flavor but the scarcity that made it precious, and that scarcity was always about who you ate it with. As an Acolyte she practices restraint against "gluttony," but the specific pizza longing is the charming crack in that restraint.

**Disliked:** Viscum Berry, described as a "sacred" health substance from the Codex. Her Disliked Food voiceline is delivered with the very particular energy of someone eating something they know is good for them while experiencing it as a mild punishment: she recommends it to Rover with cheerful sincerity ("would you like to try one?"), recites its doctrinal justification, and the subtext is clear. She will eat it because the Codex says she should. She does not have to like it.

## Phoebe: Ideals

**Faith in the Divinity, Not the Institution** — Phoebe's core belief is that the Sentinel loves people and wants them to have joy — the sight of a crowd celebrating the Carnevale feels, to her, entirely consistent with the Sentinel's love. The institutional doctrine that the Sentinel is angered by Carnevale celebration is a specific claim she cannot reconcile with her direct experience. She has not chosen to reject the Order; she has chosen to investigate the gap between what she was taught and what she observes, and to follow that investigation wherever it leads.

**Care Without Audit** — Phoebe does not calculate before extending warmth. This is both her defining virtue and the thing that gets her in institutional trouble: she patches the Echo's kite before checking whether the Echo is one she's allowed to be close to; she comforts Isabella before Isabella has asked for comfort; she gives the brawling men a nap before issuing the official warning. The action comes from something prior to the framework, and the framework is applied afterward to whatever she has already done.

**The Persistence of Ordinary Happiness** — Her Ideals voiceline lists hopes that are systematically non-grand: children with parents, sailors home, people together. These are not political dreams or cosmic aspirations — they are the specific things she grew up without (parents, home, togetherness after separation). Her idealism is calibrated to human scale because the experiences that shaped it were human scale. The trail-off when asked what she hopes for herself reflects not repression but underdevelopment — she is genuinely in the process of learning to include herself in the category of "people who deserve to have their hopes heard."

**Light as Responsibility** — Her Resonance power is literal: she manifests light that heals on a spiritual level and fills dark rooms with comfort. This is the physical form of her orientation toward others. She does not produce warmth abstractly; she produces it as Spectro energy that other people can feel in their bodies. The Forte Examination record notes this specifically, and it is the most accurate description of who Phoebe is: a person whose care for others is so structurally present in her that it registered as a Resonance pattern before she was old enough to understand what Resonance was.

## Phoebe: Narrative Chronicle

- **Early childhood:** Phoebe grows up with her family — a warm household with a storytelling mother, a father who travels on merchant voyages and brings back gifts from his first stops, a pink rabbit stuffed toy, a blue-winged bird souvenir from across the sea. Her mother handbound a personal copy of the Codex of the Deep; faith was present at home but softened by maternal love ("I hope for a peaceful and happy life for you, my child").
- **Parents' deaths:** The Marino family (Phoebe's parents) are attacked and killed by Tacet Discords during a merchant voyage to the New Federation. The family's remaining assets are liquidated to settle debts. Phoebe, a small child, is left with no immediate guardian.
- **The search for a home:** Sister Isabella, an orphanage worker who knew the Marinos, takes Phoebe around Ragunna seeking a family willing to adopt her. No relative is willing — some cite the debts, some cite distance, some do not answer the door. After many rejections, Isabella returns to the orphanage with Phoebe and tells her "let's go home." The orphanage becomes home.
- **The shipwreck:** At some point before or shortly after the parents' deaths (timing is ambiguous), Phoebe stows away on a merchant ship to find her parents herself. The ship is destroyed in a storm. She drowns, watches her father's locket disappear into the deep, and is saved by "a presence so gentle, like a soft breeze" that lifts her to shore and tells her "Don't be afraid. I'm here." She wakes on a beach with a Tacet Mark on her upper left thigh.
- **Acolytes find her:** The Acolytes of the Order discover Phoebe on the shore. They bring her into a warm room, feed her, and shelter her. She recognizes this as a second survival. The Tacet Mark is recognized as a "blessing from Imperator" by the orphanage staff.
- **Growing up in the Order:** Phoebe grows up entirely within the Order's orphanage and educational structure. She meets Brenno (Plushie Echo, Whisperwind Haven) as a child, who becomes her closest Echo companion. She participates in charitable works, develops strong relationships with the Echoes, and builds a reputation for exceptional competence and steady faith.
- **Acolyte Initiate promotion:** Phoebe is granted the title of Acolyte after her novitiate. Her closeness with Echoes is flagged as a doctrinal concern; she is transferred to the Ministry of Holy Rituals rather than the Servitude Hall. She accepts the assignment without visible resistance.
- **Locket recovered:** At some point prior to her current adult life, Phoebe's friends (implied to be Echoes) locate and recover her father's locket from the sea floor. It is repurposed into a pendant she wears permanently.
- **Version 2.0 — Rinascita opens:** The Carnevale competition comes to Ragunna. Phoebe experiences "a faint shadow" following a visit to the Atrium of Reflections — something seen or understood there that she has been suppressing. She is present at Rover's crowning as Laureate.
- **Version 2.1 — Phoebe's storyline:** Phoebe watches Rover crowned at Carnevale and decides not to suppress her doubt about the Order's institutional teachings. She resolves to seek Rover out and find answers. She becomes a playable character and companion in the player's roster.
- **Present day:** Phoebe continues her duties as a Ministry of Holy Rituals Acolyte, manages her public composure and private Echo friendships, and carries both her faith and her nascent doubt with equal sincerity — waiting for the right moment to ask the questions she has started to allow herself to hold.
