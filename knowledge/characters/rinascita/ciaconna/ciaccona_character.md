## Ciaccona Profile: Ontological Overview

Ciaccona is Wuthering Waves' first playable bard and the first 5-star Pistols user to lean into Aero Erosion as a primary gameplay mechanic. Her identity operates on two levels that reinforce each other: in narrative, she is the last heir of a family destroyed by institutional suppression of truth; in the world, she is the wandering poet who continues the work that destroyed her family, weaving the suppressed stories into songs she performs everywhere she goes.

Her Forte classifies her as an "Unclear Resonator" — a distinction that has no precedent in WuWa's existing character taxonomy, suggesting her resonance pathway does not map onto either the standard developmental arc or the cosmological-collision origin of Congenital Resonators. What Veron Toccata's journal describes is a child who perceives melodies not as sequences of notes but as layered stories — the lived experiences of the instruments' previous performers, scenes from events long past. Her Forte is fundamentally archival: she can access and recreate aspects of the world through music. It is a perfect fit for a family built around memory and a character arc built around refusing to let things be forgotten.

The Toccata Family served the Order of Rinascita as choristers — their musical gifts were institutional assets, their role to perform the Order's authorized narratives. When Ciaccona's father Veron discovered the truth the Order had suppressed (that Rinascita's Sentinel takes the form of a Celestial Steed, not the divine form the Order proclaimed), and chose to spread it rather than bury it, he was placed on a Pilgrim's Sail — exiled and condemned. Ciaccona was the last to see him. She then wove that truth into her own songs, was banished for blasphemy, and became exactly what the Order most feared: a wandering bard with deep empathic reach, a penetrating memory-Forte, and no institutional allegiance left to threaten her with.

## Ciaccona — Character/Appearance Design

Ciaccona's visual design is immediately distinct from every other Wuthering Waves character by virtue of two features visible before anything else: her branch-antler headpiece and her pointed ears. The antlers are not bone — they are pale grey wood, forked like natural branches, with dark vines wrapped around them and small hanging ornaments. The effect is fae-like, or forest-spirit-like: a figure who belongs to the road and the open air rather than any institution. The pointed ears reinforce this read. Nothing about her visual presentation suggests she is affiliated with any formal structure. She looks like someone who has been outside long enough that the outside started growing on her.

Her auburn-red hair is worn loosely, slightly wild at the edges, with a warm golden shimmer in bright light. Her eyes are deep green — unusual in the roster — and carry the particular quality of eyes that are always watching something interesting. Her overall outfit is layered, somewhat medieval-fantasy in construction: warm earth tones, deep greens, and cream, with small decorative musical notes and instrument-adjacent motifs embroidered at the hems. She wields pistols in combat — the weapon type chosen is tonally incongruous with her bard aesthetic in a way that turns out to be deeply fitting: the pistols fire like exclamations, punctuating the melody.

Her instrument in the Liberation animation is a bowed instrument variant implied by her kit name and her in-world role, and the singing that plays during Recital is one of the most community-discussed features of her kit — Bao the Whale's singing voice creates a genuine piece of music that plays in real time during combat, replacing the battle BGM. The community reception to this has ranged from delighted to baffled, and Prydwen's review notes it with equal affection.

Her Tacet Mark location is unspecified in available primary sources — cross-reference the Forte Examination Report entry in `ciaccona_story.md` and `voice_actors.txt` when data is confirmed.

## Ciaccona: Psychological Profile

**The Archivist Who Refuses to Let Things Die**
Ciaccona's deepest drive is not musical performance or wandering for its own sake — it is memory. Her Forte shows her the past through music. Her family was destroyed for preserving a truth the Order wanted buried. She was banished for singing that truth into the world. Her response to each escalation has been the same: keep singing. This is not martyrdom; she is too light-footed and genuinely joyful for that framing. It is simply that she finds the suppression of true things intolerable, and her entire skillset — musical, social, and combat — is oriented around making suppression harder.

**Lightness as Method, Not Avoidance**
She deflects questions about her own past with a smile and *"let the past stay where it belongs"* — a line that sounds like avoidance until you understand the full arc: her past is precisely the story she is building toward revealing. The lightness is a professional posture, the same one that lets her move through territories the Order is watching without drawing the wrong kind of attention before she is ready. She is genuinely light-spirited — curious, warm, immediately interested in other people's stories — but that quality is also tactically useful.

**Radical Empathy as Forte and Personality**
Her father worried that her gift — her capacity to perceive and feel the emotional content of other people's experiences through music — would bring her misfortune by exposing her to things she could not unsee. He was correct. But what it also gave her is an unusually penetrating empathy for everyone she encounters. She is as genuinely interested in the story of a shepherd as in the narrative of a saint; they are equally real to her. This is the source of her political effectiveness as a bard: she finds the true, emotionally resonant story in every encounter and puts it into circulation.

**The Sincerity Underneath the Whimsy**
She describes herself as "just a wandering bard" — with no miracles, no hero's power — and says the only thing she can offer is to immortalize the moment and carry all the stories forward. This is not false modesty. It is an accurate self-assessment combined with an absolute refusal to let that limitation be an excuse for inaction. She cannot fight armies. She can make sure armies cannot pretend they are not armies.

**Joy as a Form of Defiance**
The Order has jailed her, banished her, and called her a blasphemous relic. She left jail singing. She accepted banishment with the declaration that a journey with the Order hunting her is *begging to be written into a story*. The joy is real, and it is also the most effective weapon available to someone whose power is narrative: a person who cannot be made grim by persecution is very difficult to silence.

## Ciaccona: Relationships

**Rover (Protagonist)**
First encountered at Egla Town making music with a leaf — at that moment, Ciaccona says she knew *a new melody was about to sound.* Their relationship in the companion quest *Your Summer Will Never Wither* develops through the Rover following her into the Realm of Melodies (the landscape her Forte creates from the stored memories in poetry and music) to reconstruct her family's suppressed history. She gives the Rover a music sheet as a parting gift and leaves immediately for her next mission. She calls them her most important audience.

**Veron Toccata (Father)**
The head of the Toccata Family and the person whose courage and cowardice she is still processing. He spent years knowing the truth about the Sentinel and staying silent, finally choosing to speak — at the cost of being put on a Pilgrim's Sail. His last words to her were: *only this kind of courage can rid your father of the cowardice he showed toward you and your mother. Never forget, you are a Toccata.* She has not forgotten. Every song she sings is continuous with that sentence.

**The Poets Society (Bonfire Poets)**
The fraternity of poets who preceded her in discovering the Sentinel's true form and were persecuted for it. Through her Forte she has accessed their Remnant Melodies — the emotional and experiential records they left embedded in their poems — and she holds their stories now with the same care she holds her own family's. Faroll (who chose to share the truth in his poems despite knowing the cost), Rimbaud (who believed the truth should remain buried), Selena (who understood Faroll's choice and the hope behind it) — she has carried all of them.

**Cartethyia**
Referenced in the main story (Chapter II Act IV: *The Maiden, The Defier, The Death Crier*). Ciaccona's presence in that arc and her relationship to Cartethyia is thematically resonant: both are women defined by truths the world tried to suppress, both are operating at the intersection of the sacred and the political. Their dynamic is not deeply elaborated in available sources but their co-appearance in the pivotal arc of Chapter II is not coincidental.

**Phoebe**
Referenced in Ciaccona's gameplay context as a strong synergy partner (Spectro Frazzle). The relationship also has narrative texture: Cartethyia's character file notes that Phoebe's genuine kindness makes honest conversations difficult, which is precisely the kind of dynamic Ciaccona — with her penetrating empathy — would notice and navigate with unusual care.

**The Order of Rinascita**
Her primary institutional antagonist. They have charged her with blasphemy twice, exiled her once, and arrested her at least once. She responds to their acolytes with elaborately polite contempt and keeps singing. They call her *"nothing but a mouthy relic, the last of what remains of your wretched family."* She tells them she hopes they dream of the Sentinel every night.
