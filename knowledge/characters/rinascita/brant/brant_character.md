## Brant Profile: Basic Information

- **Name:** Brant (Chinese: 布兰特, *Bùlántè*)
- **Alias / Title:** Captain of the Troupe of Fools; "the Immortal Returned" (stage legend / wanted poster alias); Captain Brant; "a humble Fool in pursuit of freedom"
- **Identity:** A Natural Resonator and the captain of Rinascita's Troupe of Fools — a traveling performance company that sails Rinascita's seas, performing stories about freedom for everyone they encounter. He is the only male playable Resonator from Rinascita at the time of his release.

## Brant Profile: Overview

Brant is the captain of the Troupe of Fools — Rinascita's most flamboyant, most wanted, and most beloved performance company, which travels the seas aboard their ship (and, eventually, the back of a living Pliosaurus Somnii named Lario) bringing stories of freedom to audiences across the region. He grew up in Ragunna, where his father taught him about adventure through books and fossils, and was sent aboard the Pilgrim's Sail by the Order before he could learn the piano. The original captain of the Troupe placed a tricorn on his head when he was a boy and called him "Captain Brant" as a joke; the crew did the same thing in earnest years later when he was the one standing on the mast steering them clear of deadly whirlpools.

In gameplay terms Brant is a Fusion Sword Hybrid who defies easy categorization: he deals strong personal damage through aerial mid-air Basic Attack chains, provides team healing and shielding through his Forte mechanics, and buffs incoming Resonators through one of the game's most broadly applicable Outro Skills. He is also the first Fusion character in the game whose stats scale meaningfully off Energy Regen — the higher his ER, the higher his flat ATK bonus and his healing output — making him simultaneously the highest-ATK and most sustain-oriented Fusion DPS character in the roster.

## Brant — Character/Appearance Design

Brant is a young man of average height with short turquoise hair that has vivid fuchsia streaks, and fuchsia eyes to match. His Tacet Mark is located on the inner area of his left pectoral — glowing like St. Elmo's Fire during storms, according to the troupe medic's notes. His build is athletic and mobile, and his energy projects forward rather than upward: he takes up space enthusiastically rather than imposingly.

His outfit is extraordinary. The foundation is a baggy dark green shirt that splits down the middle in a black frilly outline with a bright red fiery pattern, flaring up at the top into a large collar with a bright red-and-gold checker pattern — theatrical and impractical by any measure, which is exactly the point. His shoulders are adorned with black and gold pauldrons layered over pale blue fabric with fuchsia patterning. Beneath that layer is white fabric with golden anchor-shaped pins on both sides, jewelry strung between them, frilly white and pale blue sleeves adorned with golden engravings, and various belts and extensions. He wears the tricorn hat — the captain's hat, embroidered with the Troupe of Fools' emblem — which is his most important accessory: it is the same physical hat that the original Fool's Sail captain placed on his head as a boy, and which his own crew placed on his head again when he became captain. The hat is a direct through-line between who he was told he would be and who he actually became.

His combat style uses a sword, a whip, and a pistol in different attack sequences, and his primary combat mode is aerial: he swings on a grapple between attacks, fires from mid-air, and backflips between hits. This visually translates his personality perfectly: Brant never stops moving, the stage is always everywhere he is, and the combat is performed rather than executed.

## Brant: Psychological Profile

**The Stage as Home, Not Performance** — Brant's most important distinction is that his stage persona and his true self are not in opposition. His Thoughts IV voiceline captures it: "All I think about on stage is losing myself in the role — up there, the world is my oyster. But once I step off, I'm just a minor character in the tale of Solaris." He fully inhabits every role he plays without this threatening who he is, because he has a clear sense of what "just Brant" is. The stacked masks cherished item is the icon of this: each mask is real on stage and irrelevant offstage. The face beneath them is not hidden — it is simply different, and he likes it that way.

**Charisma as Genuine Orientation** — Brant does not perform warmth. He walks into a dreary rain-soaked tavern and, within the space of one story, has turned it into a stage of imagination, handed out roses, and left everyone with Troupe of Fools tickets. This is not calculation — it is the natural expression of someone who genuinely finds people interesting and believes every situation has more potential than it is currently using. The tavern patrons do not feel manipulated by his transformation of the room; they feel included in it.

**Freedom as Praxis, Not Philosophy** — His definition of "Fool" to the children is the simplest and most precise thing he says: "anyone who pursues freedom is a Fool." He does not mean freedom as an abstract ideal but as a daily practice — the Troupe sails where it wants, performs what it wants, refuses to let the Order or Ragunna's hierarchies determine what it can do. The wanted poster is not a source of anxiety but a minor plot element in a story he is already telling better. His freedom is not the absence of consequence but the refusal to let consequence determine direction.

**Responsibility Without Solemnity** — Brant's deepest function is as a captain: he is responsible for every person in the Troupe. The Story IV sea battle shows this most clearly — he faces the colossal monster alone, tells his crew to go ahead, and charges laughing into the heart of the tempest with a grappling hook. The crew's fear, then relief, then cheering when he returns on the sea beast's head is the same pattern as his tavern performance: chaos → confidence → resolution that exceeds expectation. He carries his crew's lives without visibly carrying the weight, which is not because he doesn't feel it but because showing the weight would not help them. The captain holds the fear so the crew can hold the rope.

**Genuine Sentiment Beneath Theatrical Register** — The Story V scenes about his father's piano lessons, the sold house, and the piece he doesn't recognize from the new family — these are written with a quietness that contrasts deliberately with the bombast everywhere else. Brant stands outside his childhood home and does not go in because "it's not mine anymore." He does not frame this as loss but as completed passage: "there's no need to dwell on the past. Because once the confetti's swept up, it's time to prepare for the next revelry." The sentiment is genuine and the theatrical register is also genuine. He is not using the cheerfulness to deny the sadness; he is using it to locate the sadness correctly — as a past thing, not a current one — and move forward from there.

**The Compass That Points to People** — Brant's About Carlotta, About Zani, and About Cartethyia voicelines share a consistent quality: he sees the person behind the role. He recognizes that Carlotta will eventually be known not just as a Montelli but as herself. He observes that Zani doesn't wear masks like others do. He wonders what Cartethyia actually dreams about. His compass — the Forte designated "Flamebound Compass" — is not a navigation tool but a reading of people, and the thing it orients toward in every case is genuine self rather than social presentation. This is the deepest mirror of his own character: he is interested in who people are when no one is watching, because he knows exactly who he is.

## Brant: Relationships

**The Troupe of Fools (Family)** — Battier the fisherman, Lavito the composer, Tina the singer, Roccia the first mate and stage designer, Budd and Hymer and Katy the new recruits — the Troupe is Brant's family before it is his crew. The Story II campfire dinner scene is the most fully realized portrait of this: Brant watching them argue and play and practice while the sunset lights the sea, uncorking Nectarwine and calling it cause for celebration. He organizes their lives (polishing masks, pressing costumes, arranging props) and lets them organize themselves (returning to families, staying, taking breaks) with equal warmth.

**Roccia** — His first mate, noted separately from the crew not as first among equals but as the character whose relationship with Brant has its own texture. Brant describes her in voicelines as wise despite apparent age and an excellent stage designer. Roccia appears in Story II bringing meat bought with one of the bounties earned in Brant's name — a detail that captures their dynamic: she is doing practical things to support the Troupe's operations while he is doing theatrical things to sustain its spirit. Her loyal companion Pero appears as part of her world rather than Brant's specifically.

**Brant's Father** — A bookish man who wore glasses, taught Brant piano, and told stories about adventure using the Ammonoid fossil as a prop for a tale about unearthing the past and seeking truth. His lessons formed the foundation of Brant's orientation: books as portals to adventure, learning as active discovery rather than passive receipt. Before Brant could finish the piano lessons, the Order sent him onto the Pilgrim's Sail. He mentions wanting to visit his father's house after the Carnevale, standing outside the sold building, hearing a new family's unfamiliar piano piece, and deciding not to go in. The door not opened is the most specifically sad moment in his character stories.

**The Original Captain (Fool's Sail)** — The predecessor captain who navigated the Stygian Fjords undeterred by crashing waves, placed a tricorn on the head of an eager young boy, and called him "Captain Brant" with a playful wink. This was not a prophecy but an act of inclusion: the captain let the boy feel like a captain, and the boy remembered that feeling clearly enough to become it. The tricorn hat passed between them is the through-line between the two captains, and wearing it is Brant's most specific act of continuity.

**Rover** — Brant and Rover meet through the Rinascita main story's Carnevale arc. Brant's Birthday Wishes for Rover (an invitation to set sail and hold the helm) captures the register of their relationship: he offers Rover a place in his world rather than inserting himself into theirs, framing it as an adventure with equal standing.

**Lario (The Pliosaurus Somnii)** — The colossal sea beast Brant defeats in the storm by leaping onto its head laughing. He names it Lario, declares it "our new companion," and builds the Troupe's stage on its back. Lario is the most Brant-appropriate outcome imaginable: the monster that threatened to end them becomes the stage on which they perform.
