## Phoebe Profile: Overview

Phoebe is an Acolyte of the Order of the Deep — Rinascita's dominant religious institution — and one of the region's most quietly remarkable Resonators. She lost her parents as a young child when they were killed by Tacet Discords during a merchant voyage, and she was subsequently raised by the Order's orphanage in Ragunna after no family member was willing to take her in. The Order became her home, its Acolytes her family, and its teachings the framework through which she organized a childhood defined by grief, displacement, and the need to be useful.

Her Resonance ability, designated "Graceful Luminescence," manifests as the power to produce and shape holy light — refracting it into prismatic forms that comfort, heal, and, in combat, amplify into enormous Spectro bursts. The Order evaluated her ability as stemming from her "unwavering faith in the Divinity," which reinforced their institutional regard for her while also, ironically, nearly costing her the posting she wanted: her unusual closeness with Echoes was considered a doctrinal irregularity, and she was transferred to the Ministry of Holy Rituals rather than the Servitude Hall she might otherwise have joined.

In gameplay terms, Phoebe is the game's first dual-mode Spectro DPS, capable of functioning as a full Main DPS (Absolution mode, Heavy Attack focused) or as a Spectro Frazzle-focused Sub-DPS/Hybrid (Confession mode). She and Spectro Rover form the game's first dedicated Frazzle-synergy pair, and their combined output is among the highest team DPS available at the time of her release.

## Phoebe — Character/Appearance Design

Phoebe is a petite young woman with long golden-blonde hair that reaches past her waist, framing a face with soft features and warm golden-amber eyes that communicate her default state: attentive, kind, slightly serious. She is small in stature — described explicitly in her Story I as a "small figure" and "petite blonde Acolyte" — which combined with her composed bearing creates the characteristic impression she makes: she looks gentle until her staff comes out, and people consistently underestimate how much she can do with it.

Her outfit is the distinctive formal habit of an Order of the Deep Acolyte: deep teal-and-white layered vestments with sea-blue and gold accents, incorporating the flowing, layered silhouettes associated with Rinascita's maritime religious aesthetic. The outfit features motifs of light refraction (the design echoes the prismatic quality of her Forte) and waves, tying her visual identity to both her faith and the sea that defines both her backstory and Rinascita's culture. She carries a tall ceremonial staff, which serves both as her weapon and her symbol of office: it's the instrument she uses to summon her golden bird Echo, cast her Ring of Mirrors, and, occasionally, send rude restaurant patrons to sleep with a single gentle tap.

The most narratively significant element of her design is the Tacet Mark on her upper left thigh — noted explicitly in her Forte Examination record as the first sign that an orphanage staff member recognized as a "blessing from Imperator." It is the physical origin of her identity within the Order: the mark of resonance that brought her from a drowning child on a dark shore to someone recognized as carrying something worth cultivating.

## Phoebe: Psychological Profile

**Genuine Faith Coexisting With Nascent Doubt** — Phoebe's faith in the Order of the Deep and the Sentinel is not performance: she was raised within it entirely, it gave her structure and family when she had neither, and its framework genuinely helps her organize her experience of a world she entered traumatically. When she says a prayer, when she quotes the Codex, she means it. What makes her psychologically interesting is that her faith is precise enough to notice when the institution departs from what she actually understands the Sentinel to value. The doubts surfaced in the Atrium of Reflections, noted in her Story V, are not a crisis of faith in the Divinity — they are a crisis of trust in the institutional interpretation. These are genuinely different things, and Phoebe is careful enough to know it.

**Compassion Without Hierarchy** — Phoebe's defining behavioral quality is that she extends warmth indiscriminately: to children, to strangers, to Echoes, to the two brawling men she puts to sleep before filing their incident report. The Order's doctrine holds that the Sentinel's love is impartial and untainted by favoritism — and Phoebe's actual practice of this doctrine is more rigorous than anyone who used it to restrict her relationship with Echoes intended. She does not calibrate her kindness to institutional approval. She tries to give it freely and then manages the friction that creates with the Order as a separate task.

**Grief Metabolized Into Care** — Phoebe's childhood was genuinely terrible: her parents died when she went to find them and almost died herself; she was passed around rejection by family members before being taken to an orphanage; she spent years having nightmares reliving the shipwreck. She has metabolized this not into hardness or resentment but into an unusual sensitivity to other people's loneliness. Her instinct when Sister Isabella is visibly distressed in Story II is to give her ice cream and offer comfort — this is the gesture of a child who learned very young that adults also needed to be taken care of sometimes, and internalized it as a form of competence rather than burden.

**The Attachment Problem** — The Order's prohibition on "attachments" to Echoes is the central institutional friction in Phoebe's life. Her Thoughts III voiceline captures it exactly: she knows what the rule says, she has made a sincere attempt to rationalize it as a test of her resolve, and she still, every time she sees her Echo friends, has to physically suppress the urge to hug them. This is not defiance — she is genuinely committed to fulfilling her Acolyte duties — but it is an honest acknowledgment that the rule costs her something real, and that she has not made peace with it by pretending it doesn't. The senior Acolyte who looks the other way in Story IV is the kindest institutional response to this dilemma; Phoebe experiences it as a gift.

**Uncalculated Selflessness** — Phoebe's Ideals voiceline is structurally unusual: she lists hopes for everyone else (children near parents, sailors home safely, all people free as birds) and then is asked what she hopes for herself, and trails off with "I hope... I...". This is not evasiveness but genuine displacement — she has spent so much of her inner energy hoping on behalf of others that the question of what she wants for herself is disorienting. She has not been denied hope for herself; she has simply not had cause to practice it. The companion story (and her arc in Version 2.1 Rinascita content) is partly about her learning to extend toward herself the same quality of care she gives without calculation to everyone around her.

**The Child Who Comforted Adults** — The clearest window into Phoebe's character is the Story II scene in which a small girl hands ice cream to a weeping adult and says gently, "Don't be sad, Sister Isabella." This is Phoebe at six or seven years old, navigating rejection on behalf of herself, and her response to the adult's distress is to turn toward it rather than away. The instinct is protective rather than performative — she is genuinely trying to help. It is the template for everything she does at twenty.

## Phoebe: Relationships

**The Echoes (Brenno and friends)** — Phoebe's most emotionally significant relationships, and the most institutionally fraught. Brenno is a Plushie Echo she met in Whisperwind Haven as a child, who untangled after she freed it from a kite string, introduced her to friends, made her flower wreaths, and slipped into her bed during storms to comfort her with his warmth. The relationship predates her Acolyte training and survived it — not as open affiliation but as something she maintains privately, in defiance of the rule's spirit if not its letter. Her Thoughts I voiceline shows Brenno and friends still coming to visit her unprompted ("sorry, I just wanted to pay you a visit — didn't expect these guys to tag along"), and her distress at having to withdraw her hand from a small Echo during the orphanage celebration captures what it costs her each time she maintains the professional boundary. The Echoes are, practically speaking, the only relationships in Phoebe's life that exist entirely outside the institutional framework — and she will not let them go.

**Sister Isabella** — The woman who took Phoebe around Ragunna seeking a home after her parents' deaths, who could not find a family willing to take her, and who eventually returned to the orphanage with a lump in her throat saying "let's go back home" as if the orphanage were a genuine home rather than a second-best outcome. Isabella is mentioned in Phoebe's voice lines with warmth as one of the foundational relationships of her early life at the Order. She represents the person who witnessed Phoebe's displacement at its most raw and chose to stay.

**Rover** — Phoebe's relationship with Rover in the main story of Version 2.1 is one of genuine recognition. She watches Rover crowned Laureate at Carnevale and experiences something in that moment that unsettles her — not negatively, but productively: the sight of Rover in that light stirs the "faint shadow" from the Atrium of Reflections and makes her think, for the first time, that this is someone who might hold answers to her doubts. Her Thoughts V voiceline describes watching Rover's Laurel crowning as "etched in my heart forever." The relationship is one of trust acquired through shared navigation of Rinascita's crisis.

**The Order of the Deep (Institution)** — Phoebe's relationship with the Order is that of someone deeply embedded in an institution she genuinely loves and increasingly cannot entirely trust. The Order gave her everything — family, purpose, structure, a name for her power — and the Order's leadership also nearly derailed her promotion, restricted her most important relationships, and may be involved in the larger institutional deceptions of the Rinascita storyline. She holds the tension without resolving it into simple loyalty or simple disillusionment, which is precisely what makes her interesting.

**Brant** — Phoebe notes in her About Brant voiceline that he reminds her of an adventurer from her mother's bedtime stories: "caring for naught while the ocean thrashes around him." She observes him with affection and a slight philosophical wistfulness — he represents a mode of being (complete freedom, comedy and tragedy in equal measure) that she respects without entirely understanding.
