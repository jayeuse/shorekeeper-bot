## Ciaccona Official Introduction

*A wandering bard from Rinascita, Ciaccona. She sings not only for the Divinity, but also for the common folk. She records stories along her journeys, turning them into songs that evoke laughter, emotion, and tears in both the storytellers and the audience.*

Ciaccona, the bard of Rinascita, is free-spirited, romantic, sincere, and passionate. On her journey, she sang radiant poems of saints and heroes, weaving tales of people, miracles, turmoil, and suffering — along with the sorrow of death and farewell. She sings of tales far and wide but never once speaks of her own. When asked about her past and why she became a bard, she always smiles and replies: *"Let the past stay where it belongs."*

## Ciaccona Personality

Ciaccona is built from a paradox: the person most devoted to preserving other people's stories is the one most reluctant to tell her own. She deflects personal questions with a smile that is simultaneously genuine and a practiced deflection — she has been doing it long enough that it is both. This is not dishonesty; it is timing. She is assembling the pieces, waiting for the moment when the story is complete enough to tell, and the Rover is the audience she is slowly trusting with the full version.

Her emotional intelligence is her most defining characteristic and, as her father feared, her greatest vulnerability. She perceives the stories inside music — not metaphorically, but literally, through her Forte — and the same quality of attention that lets her reconstruct the past from a melody also means she is continuously aware of the emotional state of everyone around her. She reads grief, longing, hope, and cowardice in the grain of a sound. She found cowardice in her father's melodies long before he admitted it to her in words.

What she does with all of this is remarkable: she does not use it to manipulate, and she does not let it make her cynical. She finds each person's story genuinely interesting. She treats the shepherd's concerns with the same investment she brings to saints and heroes. This is not performance — her Forte works indiscriminately; she perceives authentically. The curiosity is real.

The political dimension of her character is the most underplayed and the most consequential: she is not merely a bard. She is an institutional memory that the Order cannot buy, bribe, or permanently silence, because her Forte is the mechanism of memory itself. Every time they jail her, she leaves with a new story. Every time they call her the last relic of a wretched family, she writes it down.

*[Secondary Journal Entry — Veron Toccata, undated]*

I do not know if this is a blessing or a curse that has befallen my child. The child has mastered numerous instruments with ease. She tells me that the melodies in the music sheets appear not just as notes to her, but also as a series of scenes: they are stories of the instruments and their performers. She took my hand and shared this perception with me, and that is when I discovered that Ciaccona can recreate aspects of the world through melodies. It is a realm of her own making. As long as she can sense these melodies, Ciaccona can transform her poetry and music into anything.

The child has an innate curiosity about everything, making it easy for her to empathize with the stories and experiences of others. I fear this gift may bring her misfortune. That she will hear the echoes that hide within the darkness of shadows.

## Ciaccona Character Story I: "The Music Room"

*She heard a sound that she had never heard before, and saw a world that was not the one she lived in.*

Ciaccona was born into the Toccata family — a family of choristers in the service of Rinascita's Order. As a young child, she wandered into a room in their home where instruments were stored, picked up a violin, and heard something no one else in the room was hearing: the instrument played back to her the sound of the people who had performed on it before. Crying babies. Life's joys. Tragedies. Voices she did not know singing songs she had never been taught.

Her father heard the door open, saw the violin in her hands, and gently took it from her with a slight look of resignation. *"I'll teach you when you're a bit older,"* he said. She turned around and waved goodbye to the violin before the door closed.

She never told anyone what she had seen that day.

From that moment she began searching for every piece of information about music she could find. The more she learned, the clearer her visions became. She came to understand what had happened: her Forte — the power to recreate the past through melodies — had manifested the first time she touched an instrument. The violin had been playing her its history. Every instrument she touched had histories inside it. The music room was an archive.

The Order saw a prodigiously talented chorister. Her father saw a child with a gift that could bring misfortune. Ciaccona saw every story that anyone had ever pressed into the grain of a note.

## Ciaccona Character Story II: "For Herself, She Shall Sing"

*"Music itself holds no emotion. It's the heart that grants it meaning. So, dear listener, tell me. What feelings did my piece evoke in you?"*

By the time Ciaccona had grown into a wandering bard, she had already been through her family's destruction once. Her father Veron had spent years knowing the truth about Rinascita's Sentinel — that its form was that of a Celestial Steed, not the divine form the Order proclaimed — and had spent those years doing nothing with that knowledge. He held it like a stone that weighed heavier each year.

Eventually, he chose to tell. He wanted everyone to know. The Order responded with the only instrument available to them: a Pilgrim's Sail. He was charged and exiled. The last time Ciaccona saw him, he told her: *"Only this kind of courage can rid your father of the cowardice he showed toward you and your mother. Never forget, you are a Toccata."*

She remembered.

Later, after she had become a wandering bard, she began to feel what was in the melodies of the poets who had come before — the Bonfire Poets, the Poets Society who had also discovered the Sentinel's true form and been persecuted for it. She accessed their Remnant Melodies through her Forte and heard the sincerity inside them, a quality she describes as like none other. She wove those stories into songs. The Order exiled her for it.

She kept singing. She planted seeds of poetry and music everywhere her journey took her. She watched the fair winds guide shepherds home. She watched clouds carry thoughts across distant lands. She watched the gentle rain, veiling tears, await the dawn. And she never once told any of the people she sang for that she was the last of a family destroyed for doing exactly this.

## Ciaccona Character Story III: "The Last of the Toccata"

*"You, Ciaccona Toccata, blaspheming against the Sentinel."*

The Order arrested her again in the companion quest arc. The acolyte's charge was the same as always: blasphemy. Her response was the same as always: politely devastating sarcasm. When they called her *"nothing but a mouthy relic, the last of what remains of your wretched family,"* she told them she hoped they dreamed of the Sentinel every night. They dragged her toward the penitentiary. She was mid-escape before they reached it.

The escape was not solo. A masked figure appeared at the precise moment needed. (She describes it in her narration as: *"A masked hero suddenly appeared, rescuing the bard right when she was about to be apprehended by the Order."*) She accepted the rescue with the same light energy she brings to everything, filed the masked figure into the ongoing story of her life, and emerged from the encounter declaring that a journey with the Order hunting her was begging to be written into a story.

What the companion quest *Your Summer Will Never Wither* reveals in full is the mechanism of her Forte in action: the Rover follows her into the Realm of Melodies — the landscape that her music constructs from the stored memories of poems and instruments — and there they encounter the Remnant Melodies of the Bonfire Poets directly. **Faroll**, who chose to share the truth in his poems despite foreseeing the cost; **Rimbaud**, who believed the truth should stay buried; **Selena**, who understood Faroll's choice and the hope that drove it. Ciaccona has been carrying all of them.

The young girl singing in the study that the Rover finds themselves following through the Realm — the one who mentions the Golden Anthem poems and the name Ciaccona Toccata — is Ciaccona herself, at an age before the exile. What the Rover witnesses is the origin of the bard who cannot talk about her own past: a child who decided that if the world would not allow the truth to be spoken plainly, she would find every other form available to make it impossible to silence.

She gives the Rover a music sheet at the end of the quest and leaves immediately for the next mission. *"Starting today, I'm no longer just a wandering bard. I've got the Order hunting me now!"* The declaration is cheerful. She means it as a promotion.

## Ciaccona: Cherished Items

**A Key (The Toccata Family's Key)**
The object Veron Toccata's journal entry references with the line: *"Yet I know that became impossible the moment she took that key."* The precise identity of the key and what it unlocks is not fully elaborated in available sources, but the context — a child who had already perceived too much through music, who had already found the archive inside the instruments — suggests it opened something her father had been trying to keep closed. The key is the moment her knowledge became irrevocable. She kept it.

**An Old Music Sheet**
A music sheet that appears in the companion quest — she gives a copy to the Rover at the quest's end as both a parting gift and a promise kept. Music sheets are the medium through which her Forte most reliably functions: they carry the emotional and experiential records of the poets who wrote them. An old music sheet from her family's archive or the Poets Society's legacy would be the most direct connection to everything she is working to preserve.

## Ciaccona: Favorite/Disliked Food

**Favorite:** Foods eaten in company, at bonfires, in the middle of stories being told — the food itself matters less than its context. Her Forte perceives the emotional histories inside things, and food at a shared meal carries the warmth of the people present. She is not primarily a food-focused character, but she is consistently described as someone who flourishes in community settings.

**Disliked:** Meals in institutional settings — the kind served by organizations that have something to gain from your compliance. She has eaten enough Order-adjacent hospitality to know the flavor of hospitality as leverage.

## Ciaccona: Ideals

**Passion — Every Story Deserves a Singer**
Her foundational commitment is the most democratic possible version of a bard's purpose: no story is beneath preservation, and no audience is too small. She sings for the Divinity and for shepherds with equal investment. This is not an affectation — her Forte works indiscriminately, and what it shows her is that every instrument, every melody, every person who has ever pressed feeling into sound was worth the notes they left behind.

**Resonance — Truth As Music**
She does not argue with the Order. She sings. This is not because she lacks arguments — her improvised verbal combat with the acolyte who arrested her demonstrates considerable facility with direct confrontation — but because she understands that arguments can be refuted and songs cannot. A song that makes someone feel something true has already done its work before the listener has decided whether to resist it. The Golden Anthem will echo across Ragunna's skies again. That is not a hope. It is a schedule.

**Gratitude — The Poets Society**
She carried the Bonfire Poets' stories before she ever found the Rover. Faroll's choice to share the truth despite knowing the cost; Rimbaud's grief at the idea of the truth remaining buried forever; Selena's understanding that hope was the thing driving all of it — she held those stories for years without an audience large enough to receive them. The companion quest is the first time she has been able to share them with someone capable of actually entering the Realm of Melodies and meeting the poets directly. The gratitude she feels is toward the architecture of the world that finally sent her someone with that capacity.

**Acceptance — "Let the Past Stay Where It Belongs"**
This is her apparent motto and is, in context, not avoidance but precision: she is not saying the past should be forgotten. She is saying that the past lives in the music, where it belongs — it is preserved, accessible, and safe from the Order's capacity to suppress it. The wandering bard does not need to carry her history on her face. She has it somewhere better.

## Ciaccona: Narrative Chronicle

- **Early childhood:** Born into the Toccata Family — a family of choristers affiliated with Rinascita's Order. First activates her Forte as a young child in the family music room by picking up a violin and hearing its history. Does not tell anyone. Begins methodically researching everything about music.
- **Order recognition:** The Order identifies Ciaccona's exceptional ability and designates her as destined to be the Toccata Family's most radiant chorister. Forte examination conducted; classified "Unclear Resonator"; results show stability and high Resonant Criticality. Veron Toccata records his apprehension in private journal entries.
- **The Key:** Ciaccona takes a key — an act Veron records as the moment her happiness became permanently impossible. The nature of what it unlocked has not been fully revealed in available sources.
- **Her father's exile:** Veron Toccata discovers (or reconfirms from family knowledge) the suppressed truth about Rinascita's Sentinel — that it takes the form of a Celestial Steed, not the form the Order proclaims. He chooses to spread this truth publicly. The Order places him on a Pilgrim's Sail. His last words to Ciaccona are recorded in the story. Ciaccona does not see him again.
- **Becoming a wandering bard:** After her father's exile, Ciaccona leaves institutional life and begins wandering Rinascita. She accesses the Remnant Melodies of the Poets Society through her Forte — absorbing Faroll's, Rimbaud's, and Selena's experiences and testimonies about the Sentinel's true form.
- **The Order's banishment:** Ciaccona weaves the Poets Society's stories and her family's history into her own songs — the Golden Anthem tradition. The Order charges her with blasphemy and banishes her. She continues performing.
- **Version 2.2 — Story introduction:** First appears in the main story; the Rover encounters her at Egla Town making music with a leaf. She identifies the Rover as the beginning of a new melody.
- **Companion Quest — Your Summer Will Never Wither (Version 2.3):** Ciaccona is arrested by the Order for blasphemy. A masked figure assists her escape. She leads the Rover into the Realm of Melodies — the experiential landscape generated by her Forte from the accumulated memories in poetry and music. The Rover encounters the Remnant Melodies of the Bonfire Poets directly: Faroll, Rimbaud, Selena. They also find a young Ciaccona Toccata in the memory-landscape. The full history of the Toccata Family's destruction and the Poets Society's suppression is revealed. Ciaccona receives a new mission — an "escape mission," she calls it. She gives the Rover a music sheet and departs.
- **Main Story appearances:** Ciaccona appears in Chapter II Act IV (*The Maiden, The Defier, The Death Crier*) and Act X/XI, suggesting her arc extends through the Cartethyia-centered main narrative of Chapter II.
- **Version 2.3 Phase 2 — Playable Release (May 21, 2025):** Ciaccona becomes a playable character. Her Recital Liberation, Ensemble Sylphs, and the singing BGM replacement during combat are immediately community-notable. Her kit encodes her entire identity: a persistent off-field presence that keeps playing even when she is not on screen, Ensemble Sylphs that complete her songs even when she is interrupted, and a Forte Heavy Attack named Quadruple Downbeat that pulls everything in before delivering the final verse.
