## Cantarella Official Introduction

*"Fisalia, Cantarella, the Bane. A mysterious noblewoman, her beauty as captivating as it is perilous. She resides in a crown-like castle perched atop the mountain, where illusory dreams flow like streams, meticulously spun by her own hands."*
— Official Website (wutheringwaves.kurogames.com)

Her archive summary: *"The Fisalia family's thirty-sixth head, Cantarella Fisalia. She locks your gaze with eyes as deep as the ocean, where dark tides seem to surge and swirl. For a moment, you think you glimpse something in the ocean's depths — but you are unsure if it is the shadow of a giant sea creature... or the tail feathers of the Celestial Stede."*

Her own framing of the central ambiguity: challenging players to decide if she is *"the Bane of the wicked or the pure."*

## Cantarella Personality

Cantarella's most immediately legible quality is composure — the kind that has been arrived at through cost rather than born into, which is the more impressive variety. She is the thirty-sixth head of a family whose tradition has killed every head before her, including through their own hands, and she carries this fact with the particular stillness of someone who has thought about it thoroughly and chosen, deliberately, not to be destroyed by it.

The five character stories are the fullest portrait available of who she actually is, and they are unusual: they are structured as a round table meeting between several distinct internal voices, not as a conventional linear narrative. The meeting is called by a figure who is, by position, Cantarella — but the voices around her turn out to be facets of herself: the imperious authoritarian, the cold pragmatist, the methodical scientist, the free-spirited youngest one. She runs the meeting the way she runs the family: she gives every voice time, records every answer fairly, reaches a decision at the end, and thanks everyone for their invaluable opinions.

The final story's gesture — waking from an afternoon nap and drinking ordinary tea — is the most precisely observed moment in the available archive. Not the dream tea, not the alluring purple varieties. The ordinary tea she bought in Ragunna this morning. It tastes like *"a breeze in the countryside, of dew drops hanging on fresh leaves."* The Tacet Mark in her mouth is no longer restless. She chose reality. She kept the dream. She made them coexist.

The report structure is as revealing as its content. The most dangerous information is redacted by system failure rather than human decision — which means the δ waveform exceeds whatever the documentation system is capable of containing. The γ waveform being *"short in amplitude and seemingly underdeveloped"* maps precisely to the youngest, most vulnerable voice in the round table: the part of Cantarella that watched birds from windows and dreamed of fairytales, described as short and underdeveloped in medical terms. The α waveform's wild fluctuations correspond to the imperious authoritarian who advocates for domination. The β waveform's extreme stability corresponds to the cold pragmatist. The classification *"Top Secret"* is Sebastian's protection of Cantarella — this information is not for the public archive.

Apothecary Rosemary's nervous addendum (*"I hope I didn't make any mistakes"*) is the document's single moment of overt humanity, preserved in the official record because Cantarella approved it. Either she did not notice it, or she chose to keep it. Given what the five stories reveal about her character, she almost certainly chose to keep it.

## Cantarella Character Story I: "Round Table"

The light filtered through the stained glass, casting a speckled pattern across the crimson sofa.

Dim lamps flickered softly in the misty purple room. Around the round table sat several indistinct figures. Some lounging in the light, others half-immersed in the shadows. The lady who arranged this gathering spoke first. She had long, silky purple hair, and her indigo eyes, veiled in a soft mist, gave her an ethereal, dreamlike quality. She absentmindedly toyed with the sapphire pendant on her chest — a habit when lost in thought.

*"I've gathered everyone today to discuss one thing: where should we stay? In the 'Illusory Dream,' or in reality?"*

*"Since we've been wandering the edge of dreams, should we remain here, in this illusion, where everything is familiar, light and delicate as bubbles? Or should we step forward, into reality, where we might face the unknown… but also see sights we've never known?"*

A lazy but resolute voice cut through her words. *"How weak of you, Cantarella. You may be head of the family now, but from what I see, you're far from being capable."*

A figure emerged from the shadows — a woman with long hair cascading like a waterfall, wearing the same sapphire pendant. She carried grace similar to Cantarella's, but with the undeniable weight of experience and authority. Her deep purple eyes glinted with an unquestionable edge.

*"This meeting is pointless. Just pour the 'Velvet Dream' into the waterfall behind the castle, and let all of Ragunna fall into a dream under your control. That way, we will be queen wherever we go."*

*"Invalid answer. Or, if you insist, it will be counted as a vote for 'Illusory Dream.' Though it sounds a little wild and impractical, I'll still write it down for fairness. This is our discussion, after all."* A young girl sitting beside them answered, opening a notebook full of strange doodles. She found a small space nearly buried under potion recipes and seriously drew a neat line.

*"I abstain,"* she said casually. *"This discussion probably won't change anything. You already have your answer, don't you? For me, it doesn't matter where we stay."* She closed the notebook with a soft snap and shook out her white shawl. Light caught her blue, hazy eyes.

*"Why can't we just keep it simple and follow our hearts? Cantarella, you like watching the birds outside the window and long for a life of freedom, right? Then just do what feels right,"* said the youngest one, sprawled on the sofa, her hands lazily chasing the dust motes dancing in the light.

*"This is an important decision and should be discussed seriously,"* Cantarella replied, her voice tinged with a hint of helplessness. *"Let's start with you then. Please explain your choice and the reasoning behind it."*

The first story establishes the round table without yet explaining what it is. A moderating figure, three or four voices with distinct personalities (imperious authority, methodical scientist, free spirit, carefree youngest), and the question framing the discussion: illusion or reality? The most structurally significant detail is the youngest figure's observation: *"You already have your answer, don't you?"* She is right. Cantarella does. The round table is not a genuine deliberation about an unknown outcome — it is the formal process by which she arrives at, and ratifies, the decision she has already made in her heart.

## Cantarella Character Story II: "Thorned Crown"

The deep purple, venomous crown, when first placed upon the brow, brings a sharp sting. Yet, the blood that seeps from the forehead nourishes it, making it shine ever brighter. Soaked in the blood and tears of countless souls, this crown of toxic thorns rises from the depths of the sea, symbolizing those who work silently against the chaos to support the weight of the Celestial Stede's tail feathers.

Such is the glory of the Fisalia family, and its inescapable fate.

But what of each member of the family? In the dark, deep sea, they suffer the gnawing whispers, until the faintest glimmer of true faith in their hearts fades, lost to the abyss. The next in line to wear the crown will take up the duty, and so the cycle endures, unbroken, with no beginning or end.

Fragrant yet toxic blooms, colorless flavorless tears of poisons, twisted organs and venomous breaths, neurotoxins, biotoxins, metallic toxins…

The poisons and toxins the Fisalias so pride themselves on and the abilities birthed from them will inevitably be undone by the whispers. In the end, they only come in handy when used *"to seek the end of life, either for the Fisalias themselves or their kin."*

This had long been the norm until *"I"* developed the **Illusory Dream**. When the thorned crown burrowed into my flesh, the blood flew in strange, shimmering hues. Black tea steeped in mercury tasted like rich, honeyed nectar, while scones soaked in Jellyrose juice had a delicate sweetness. The heat in my throat was searing, as if hastily swallowing an entire cherry, and the suffocating sensation was like drifting in an endless sea… Slowly, the pain faded, and both body and mind became light as air. The bottles of poisons shattered on the floor, and our expressions turned relieved. The whispers were powerless in the world woven by the sea of dreams, for it gently dissolved all discord. Amidst the colorful waves, the once harsh thorned crown was softened… So soft that it formed delicate folds, resting lightly on my head. It became light and pure, like the tail feathers of the Celestial Stede.

*That's why I cast my vote for "Illusory Dream."* After all, what harm is there in this illusion? Have we not all wandered here for so long? Pour the "Velvet Dream" into the waterfall behind the castle, and let the whole of Ragunna fall into a dream shaped by my hand… Rest assured, this is but a joke. I do not seek to control but only wish for all to live more peacefully.

You see, the "Illusory Dream" flows within our veins, and whether we like it or not, it's a permanent and inseparable part of us.

This is the imperious voice's story — the α waveform's account — and it is the one that comes closest to articulating the genuine case for the Illusory Dream. The Fisalia curse, the whispers, the cycle with no beginning or end: the Illusory Dream dissolved all of this, softened the crown, gave the family something other than death for the first time in thirty-six generations. The vote for Illusory Dream is the vote for what has already saved them. The joke about the waterfall — *"Rest assured, this is but a joke"* — is the α waveform's single moment of self-awareness; it knows its own inclination toward domination and names it before it can be named by anyone else.

## Cantarella Character Story III: "Jellyfish Lamp"

In the dimly lit room, heavy with a peculiar scent, a faint yellow light illuminated an old notebook and a strange cauldron. The young woman, wrapped in a white shawl, scribbled notes in the book while casually tossing ingredients into the cauldron. The smoke rising from it darkened her clothes, leaving patches of black and purple. Her once-clean apron was now speckled, but she wiped her face carelessly, unfazed by the mess.

*Distilled water, peppermint, castor bean, barnacle fish leaves, rosemary… The scent was fresh, the toxicity mild, with soothing and analgesic effects. It could treat the common Infantile Gray Matter syndrome seen recently… I'll write this recipe on a small note and leave it for Chloe later.*

*Hilo grass, sea okra, barnacle fish leaves, sea apple juice… The scent was unpleasant, and the toxicity potent, though it could counteract some nerve paralysis reactions. Hmm, but the side effects… Never mind, I'll keep it.*

*Sea okra mucilage, barnacle fish leaves dried and crushed, a splash of sea apple juice, a touch of Jellyrose juice… The toxicity of Jellyrose is too strong. Effective, but too risky.*

*Crush the barnacle fish leaves, drizzle with Violacas juice, mix with sea okra mucilage, add a bit of sea apple juice, and let it ferment in a glass vial… Done. Once consumed, it soothes the spirit and neutralizes most poison reactions. I should distribute some to everyone to get through the trials in the coming days. And the name… okay, I'll call it Velvet Dream.*

The ingredients, processing method, sequence of preparation, and environmental temperature… Handle each step at the right moment, and she can brew the perfect elixir. She believes the same applies to life. At the right moment, with the right steps, most problems can be solved. No matter how illogical the rules or how brutal the situation, there is always a way to find the right approach.

Therefore, she stepped into the darkness with the lamp, facing her fears. The abyss mocked her — reclusive, weak, incapable of adapting. Its taunting howls echoed through the corridors, the **twisted frequencies of past family heads** surging forward, engulfing her. Serious, rigid, pale, gloomy, silent… They knew her too well, able to predict her every move and trap her effortlessly.

But still, she played her very first and last trick as she drank a large dose of Velvet Dream in advance. The moment the jellyfish's poisonous thorns pierced her skin, she pressed the **Jellyrose Medusa to her head**. The violent toxins clashed within her veins, nearly tearing her apart. Her soul rolled between life and death, on the verge of spilling out through her burning throat. She couldn't tell if it was blood, tears, or sweat mixed with the remnants of the potion… It dripped onto the almost extinguished lamp.

A small jellyfish wobbled out from the faint light. When she raised her eyes, the darkness lifted, and her blurred vision filled with colorful, vivid illusions.

*It worked.*

So, like she said, any situation can be solved with the right steps at the right time, even if you're just one part of the process. Since then, she has remained in **a perpetual state between life and death, between dreams and reality**. She often catches glimpses of lingering illusions, like the girl in the red hat. Who is she? Is it her? Or someone else? She can't figure it out.

*Anyway, I'll abstain from voting. It doesn't matter whether it's in a dream or reality. I've been used to it, and I can survive anywhere.*

This is the methodical scientist's story — the γ waveform's account — and it is the story of how Cantarella survived the trials. The γ voice is described as *"short in amplitude and seemingly underdeveloped"* in the Overclock Report; in the narrative it is the voice of someone who approaches every problem with recipes, sequences, and careful application of the right ingredient at the right moment. She survived by engineering a way through: Velvet Dream absorbed first (to buffer the shock), Jellyrose Medusa pressed to her head while the previous poison was still active (to overwhelm the trial's attacking frequencies with internal chaos they could not predict). It worked. The cost was permanent: she now exists between life and death, between dreams and reality. She abstains from the vote because she genuinely cannot distinguish between the two states any more. Both feel the same. She can survive either.

The *"girl in the red hat"* she sees in the lingering illusions is a mystery unresolved in available sources — an internal presence she cannot identify as herself or another, possibly the δ waveform whose documentation generated system errors.

## Cantarella Character Story IV: "Uncaged Bird"

Since she could remember, she had always been in that big, empty house. She would walk from one end to the other, knowing every tile on the floor and every potion bottle on the shelves. The house was gray, dark, and quiet. The only time it ever felt alive was when a bird flew past the window. She would watch its white wings flutter up and down until it became nothing more than a tiny dot in the sky. Beyond that, the sky turned a clear blue, and the trees shone a bright green. It was such a beautiful sight.

That was when she would wonder… what was happening in that thick forest? Maybe a princess was having an adventure.

She imagined a clever princess who knew how to spot a poisoned apple. When a snake chased her, a little bird would swoop in to save her, carrying her high into the sky. There, she would find a girl in red shoes who couldn't stop dancing and save her. Then, they would dive into the sea to rescue a siren who was on the verge of turning into foam. In the end, the bird would carry them all, flying as far as it could until it could fly no more, finally landing in a castle… But what was the castle like? She only hoped it wasn't like the manor she lived in — big and boring.

*Sorry, that probably sounds all over the place, doesn't it?* Well… Mother and Father always said that girls from the Fisalia family didn't need to read silly fairytales or have strange ideas like that. They were supposed to read books — books like *The Complete Guide to Poison Recipes*, *The Fisalia Family's Secret Elixirs*, and *108 Poisons You Never Knew About*. They were interesting, but after reading them too many times, they became dull.

Still, Mother and Father insisted that she memorize everything so that one day she could save herself, or perhaps even become a Maiden or the family's leader. It sounded like a huge responsibility. She didn't really understand it yet, but she did what they told her.

They were always busy, always rushing around. Whenever they left, they locked the door extra tight and checked it over and over again. She never understood why they grew busier and thinner. And why did they drink so much *"water"* and mumble strange words?

She had learned to say those words too: *"It's the glory and fate of the Fisalias."*

Eventually, she grew older and had to move to a different house. When she left, Mother and Father looked proud, but their eyes were full of tears.

*"Indeed, milord, our child has great talent, especially in brewing poisons."*
*"Yes, becoming a candidate for the trials is the glory and fate of the Fisalias."*

That was the first time she ever left home. As she stepped outside, her footsteps startled the crows perched on the dead tree near the gate. They flapped their black wings and took off, flying toward a strange castle in the distance.

*Oh, sorry, I went off track again. What I meant to say was... after spending so long flying through dreams, you start to miss the feeling of your feet on the ground. Sure, you might step into mud or get pricked by sharp stones, but there was something comforting about taking each step on solid earth.*

So, when it came time to vote, she chose reality — *The Other Side of the Dream*, as the adults liked to call it. They always used fancy terms like that. But deep down, you already knew what your heart truly wanted, didn't you? Not just in dreams, but in reality, too. You could open the door, step outside, and go with everyone. Just fly alongside the white birds and chase after the happily-ever-after ending for everyone.

This is the youngest voice's story — the voice described as the free spirit who sprawled on the sofa chasing dust motes — and it is the most overtly childhood account. The fairytale she imagined as a child is a multi-story rescue narrative (saving the poisoned apple princess, the girl in red shoes who can't stop dancing, the siren turning into foam) that ends with a bird carrying everyone to a castle that hopefully isn't big and boring. The fairytale is her aspirational self-portrait: the one who rescues, carries, and takes everyone somewhere better.

Her vote for reality is not a rejection of the dream but the acknowledgment that the feet want ground. The mud and sharp stones are named specifically — she is not naive about what reality involves. She is choosing it anyway, because *"there was something comforting about taking each step on solid earth."*

## Cantarella Character Story V: "Lucid Dream"

In the misty purple room, voices blended together — the majestic and enchanting, the cold and resolute, and the genuine and innocent. They intertwined, forming a dreamlike symphony. The sapphire light shimmered with quiet authority, the pages of a book rustled softly, and the scent of freshly baked pastries mingled with the warmth of the room, creating a sweet, ethereal atmosphere.

Cantarella lifted the delicate teapot and poured the tea — a change from the usual. No longer the alluring purple hues, but a cup of tea with a refreshing clarity.

*"Thank you for your invaluable opinions,"* she said, setting the teapot down. The fresh aroma filled the room.

*"Now, I've made up my mind."*

*"I will continue to wear the crown of thorns, for the poison has already sunk deep into my bones, and the dream is a part of me I cannot remove."*

*"I will keep brewing potions, for they are needed not just by the Fisalias, but by all in struggle."*

*"I will step outside and follow the flight of birds, into the mountains and the streets. The whispers that once trapped us are gone, and now we are free."*

*"I am still within the dream, but I will strive to feel the ground beneath my feet… In that fleeting moment between dream and reality, I will hold on to what is real."*

Cantarella took a deep breath, slowly opening her eyes. She had just woken from a brief afternoon nap, stepping out from a hazy yet lucid dream. The sunlight at three in the afternoon filtered through the glass window, casting a quiet glow over her lonely figure in the room. The only sounds left were the gentle bubbling of the tea and her steady breathing.

She picked up her cup and took a sip. The fresh aroma soothed the restless **Tacet Mark in her mouth**. It no longer draws power from illusion or pain. This was the tea she had bought earlier this morning in Ragunna — just ordinary tea. The faint sweetness and subtle fragrance reminded her of a breeze in the countryside, of dew drops hanging on fresh leaves…

*So, this was the taste of reality.*

The final story is the waking. The round table existed inside an afternoon nap — the five stories are structured as a dream within a dream, a fiction within a fiction, a council held in the space between consciousness and unconsciousness that is Cantarella's native territory. The decisions reached inside the nap are real decisions; the resolution she announces at the table is the one she carries out when she opens her eyes.

The ordinary tea is the story's most important object. It is not the purple Fisalia tea, not a potion, not a brew of ingredients carefully calibrated for effect. It is tea she bought this morning in Ragunna. The Tacet Mark in her mouth — which has been described elsewhere as *"restless"*, as drawing power from illusion or pain — is soothed by it. The taste of reality, which she has spent her entire adult life choosing between and struggling to identify, turns out to taste like a breeze in the countryside and dew drops on fresh leaves. Simple. Clean. Present.

## Cantarella: Cherished Items

**Coral Parasol**
*"The parasol carried by Cantarella. Its round canopy resembles a soft, plump jellyfish with long, graceful tentacles that seem to float effortlessly in the air. The ribs of the parasol are crafted from deep-sea coral, meticulously shaped into perfect curves that gleam with a subtle glow, both beautifully delicate and unyieldingly strong. At the handle, a sapphire is embedded, a symbol of the unshakable authority of the family head. Violence has never been Cantarella's first choice, but when forced to defend herself, she merely spins her parasol, and in an instant, the canopy radiates brilliant light. From it, a mesmerizing swarm of jellyfish unfurls, drifting into a phantasmal sea of illusion. Drawing her enemies into this enchanting yet perilous sea of dreams is the signature art of Cantarella. Like its owner, the parasol may appear soft and fluid, yet when the need arises, it is always indestructible."*

The parasol is both her primary weapon and a philosophical self-portrait in object form. The jellyfish canopy mirrors the Illusory Dream's mechanism exactly: beautiful, disorienting, and capable of death only through prolonged exposure. The coral ribs being *"beautifully delicate and unyieldingly strong"* mirrors the Fisalia tradition: beautiful on the surface, structurally indestructible in practice. The sapphire at the handle mirrors the sapphire pendant she wears — the same stone, the same authority, the same weight, distributed between her person and her weapon so that she is never without the mark of what she carries. The final line — *"Like its owner, the parasol may appear soft and fluid, yet when the need arises, it is always indestructible"* — is the most precise one-sentence description of Cantarella available in the entire archive.

**Cauldron of Sweet Dreams**
*"The cauldron used by Cantarella, the Sea Witch, for potion brewing. The sapphire adorned on it marks Cantarella's authority as the head of the Fisalias. She often allows her whims to guide her, carelessly tossing in unexpected ingredients, such as jellyfish tentacles, fish scales, tears from the heartbroken, and even the murmurs that linger in the air. Whatever she adds, the result is always a pot of sweet dreams that dissolves the bitterness of reality, gently soothing those lost in the abyss."*

The cauldron's ingredients list is the exact inversion of the Fisalia tradition's poison recipe books from Story IV: instead of toxins precisely measured and carefully calibrated, Cantarella tosses in *"tears from the heartbroken"* and *"murmurs that linger in the air"* based on her whims, and the result is always sweet dreams. The carefulness of Story III's scientist (who knew exactly which ingredient concentration was too risky) is replaced by the freedom of the cauldron's use. The second sapphire mark — paralleling the pendant and the parasol handle — is here too. The authority of the family head is present in everything she makes and carries. She cannot escape it. She has stopped trying to.

**Potion Vial**
*"Cantarella always carries this potion vial, adorned with a sapphire as bright as the one she wears as a mark of her authority. Inside, she keeps a special brew, one she carefully prepares to ease weariness and calm nightmares. However, ever since you brought her away from the Sea of Ghosts, the nightmares have ceased. Now, this vial is imbued with her rare sincerity and tenderness, like a soft light piercing the abyss, reflecting the gentle murmurs hidden in the rolling waves. It is now a symbol of the trust Cantarella has placed in you, the one who has navigated the dark tides and reached the depths for her."*

The Potion Vial is the most temporally specific of the three Cherished Items: it documents a change. Before the main story events, it contained a brew to ease weariness and calm nightmares. After Rover brought her away from the Sea of Ghosts, the nightmares stopped. The vial is now *"imbued with her rare sincerity and tenderness"* rather than the sleep-calming potion. It is still carried — the sapphire authority mark is still there — but its contents have changed. The third sapphire in the collection (pendant, parasol, cauldron, vial) marks this transformation: what she keeps close, and the reason she keeps it, has shifted from self-protection to trust. *"Rare sincerity and tenderness"* — both words are emphasized. Not just tenderness, not just sincerity, but specifically the rare versions of them: the ones that Cantarella does not easily extend.

## Cantarella: Voice Lines

**Thoughts: I**
*"I've been wondering lately: Am I the dreams that I dream, or am I the soul that dreams? {PlayerName}, do you perhaps have an answer?"*
The founding philosophical question of her entire characterization, addressed to Rover directly. She does not offer her own answer — she asks theirs. This is the moderating figure at the round table, still asking the question that the round table was called to address, still interested in voices beyond her own.

**Thoughts: II**
*"Ragunna's Carnevale is quite the spectacle, don't you think? All those beautiful masks, the singing, the dancing, and the laughter... I envy those who can truly revel in it. As the head of the Fisalia family, I must maintain my dignity and composure at all times. But I'll tell you a secret — sometimes I do sneak out to watch it all from the shadows."*
The Carnevale voice line is the most candid of the available thoughts lines: she cannot participate openly, so she watches from the shadows. The city she was raised to govern from behind closed doors is still a place she sneaks out to look at, in secret, from a distance. The envy is named straightforwardly — *"I envy those who can truly revel in it"* — which is a different kind of honesty than her usual composure allows.

**Thoughts: III**
*"You know, as a child, I used to watch the birds flying past my window for hours. I would imagine soaring alongside them, free from all responsibilities. Of course, that's nothing but a childhood fantasy. But sometimes, I still wonder... what it would feel like to just fly away."*
Story IV (Uncaged Bird) is this voice line expanded to full narrative. The fantasy and the wondering are not abandoned by the adult Cantarella; they exist alongside the composure and the responsibility. The wondering continues. She chose reality in the fifth story — but the wondering about flying is part of that reality, not a thing the choice resolved.

**Thoughts: IV**
*"I've been told that my potions can make people experience their deepest desires or their worst fears. But what do I experience when I use my own abilities? Well, let's just say... it's complicated."*
*"Complicated"* is doing significant work here. The Forte Examination Report says the Illusory Dream can be a double-edged sword that often causes harm to the user; the Overclock Report documents irreparable damage to both mind and body. The word *"complicated"* is Cantarella's characteristically composed understatement for something that the available medical documents can only partially contain.

**Thoughts: V**
*"I am the thirty-sixth head of the Fisalia family, yet I sometimes feel as though I am merely a fleeting dream — one that will vanish before long. But now, I know what I am: I am Cantarella Fisalia. And I will do whatever it takes to ensure that the Fisalia family, and all those who depend on us, can have a future."*
The most direct statement of her identity and commitment available in the voice lines. The *"fleeting dream — one that will vanish before long"* acknowledges the unstable waveform, the missing frequency record, the permanent state between life and death established in Story III. What follows is not a denial but a decision: *I am Cantarella Fisalia.* The name is both the lineage and the person. She will do whatever it takes.

**Hobby**
*"In my spare time, I enjoy experimenting with new potions. It's a rather... solitary pursuit. Not many people appreciate having a tasting volunteer. Well, I suppose not many would volunteer to be an experimental subject, either."*
The humor in the Hobby voice line is dry and precise: the solitary pursuit framed first in terms of the experimenter's loneliness, then reframed in terms of the reasonableness of potential volunteers' reluctance. She understands both sides of the equation. She prefers the work regardless.

**Favorite Food**
*"I have a fondness for dishes that combine sweet and savory flavors. The contrast is rather... intriguing, don't you think? Much like life itself."*
The favorite food answer becomes a metaphor before she finishes the sentence, which is her characteristic mode: *"the contrast is rather... intriguing, don't you think? Much like life itself."* She cannot taste food without tasting what the food means. This is not pretension; it is the condition of someone who has been living between dream and reality long enough that the literal and the symbolic have become difficult to separate.

**Disliked Food**
*"I dislike foods that are overwhelmingly bitter without any redeeming qualities. Bitterness serves a purpose, of course — it's a warning, a form of protection. But when it's all there is... well, that strikes me as rather pointless, doesn't it?"*
The disliked food is bitterness without purpose — *"when it's all there is"* — which is also a precise description of the Fisalia family tradition before she changed it. Bitterness that has no sweetness to balance it, no dream to dissolve it, no Illusory Dream to soften the crown. Pure bitterness is what she has spent her entire life working against.

**About Zani**
*"Zani is a remarkable individual. She possesses an inner strength that is both admirable and reassuring. I believe she will go far, regardless of the path she chooses."*
The assessment is brief and genuine. Cantarella recognizing *"inner strength"* in Zani is consistent with what she knows about the cost of strength: she has paid for hers at significant personal price, and she can identify the same quality in others because she knows what it actually looks like under the surface.

**About Phoebe**
*"Phoebe is... interesting. Her faith and dedication are undeniable, yet she approaches divine matters in a rather unconventional way. I respect that about her. It takes a certain kind of courage to question what others simply accept."*
The respect for questioning received wisdom is consistent with Cantarella's entire arc: she inherited a tradition that killed every head of her family and chose to change it, which required exactly the kind of courage she is describing.

**Ideals**
*"My ideal world? One where the Fisalia family no longer needs to carry the burden of the thorned crown — where we can live freely, without the weight of ancient curses or the shadows of the past. I know it may sound naive, but I believe it is worth striving for."*
The ideals voice line is the only place in the available sources where she names the goal directly: *"where we can live freely."* She pre-emptively defends it against charges of naivety — *"I know it may sound naive"* — which is the same move the Thorned Crown story makes (voting for Illusory Dream but noting that the Velvet Dream waterfall plan is *"but a joke"*). She is aware of how her hopes sound from outside. She holds them anyway.

## Cantarella: Narrative Chronicle

- **[Fisalia Childhood — Porto-Veno Castle adjacent manor]** Cantarella grows up in the large, gray, quiet Fisalia family home; her parents are always busy and growing thinner; they lock the doors tight when they leave; she memorizes *The Complete Guide to Poison Recipes* and its companion volumes as instructed; she watches birds from the window; she imagines fairytale adventures; she learns the phrase *"It's the glory and fate of the Fisalias"* and repeats it without fully understanding it
- **[Selection as Trial Candidate]** Cantarella is selected as a candidate for the Fisalia family trials; her parents look proud but weep; she leaves home for the first time; she is brought to a different house — presumably Porto-Veno Castle or an adjacent institution — to undergo preparation
- **[The Trials — The Jellyfish Lamp Incident]** Cantarella faces the trials: the twisted frequencies of past Fisalia family heads attack her, knowing her predictable patterns; she engineers a solution using her knowledge of poisons (Velvet Dream for protection, Jellyrose Medusa for disruption); she survives the violent internal clash of competing toxins; the Forte awakens in the process; the first jellyfish wobbles out from the faint lamp light; she passes the trials; she is left in a permanent state between life and death, between dreams and reality
- **[Development of the Illusory Dream]** Cantarella develops the Illusory Dream — the hallucinogenic toxin that, at its most refined, induces hallucinations without causing any physical harm; this is the first time in Fisalia history that a family head's Forte is not oriented toward lethal poison; the Velvet Dream is also formulated during this period as a broad-spectrum poison neutralizer with soothing properties; she brews the Velvet Dream for distribution to family members to get through difficult periods
- **[Ascension to Thirty-Sixth Family Head]** Cantarella becomes the thirty-sixth matriarch of the Fisalia family; she takes residence in Porto-Veno Castle, the crown-like structure perched atop the mountain where illusory dreams flow like streams; she receives the title *"The Bane"* and the epithet *"The Sea Witch"* from the public; Sebastian becomes her butler and begins maintaining mental health management records; Apothecary Rosemary is periodically invited to conduct the public-level Forte Examination
- **[Former Blessed Maiden]** At some point in her adult life, Cantarella served as the Blessed Maiden of Imperator — a role within Ragunna's Acolyte-adjacent institutional hierarchy that involves specific ceremonial and possibly theological functions; the timing and nature of her departure from this role is not documented in detail in available sources; verify with the Fandom Wiki
- **[The Sea of Ghosts — Main Story Events]** During the main story events of Version 2.2, Rover intervenes in circumstances that lead to Cantarella being brought away from the Sea of Ghosts — a location or state described in the Potion Vial cherished item; after this intervention, her nightmares cease; the vial's contents change from a nightmare-calming brew to something she describes as *"rare sincerity and tenderness"*; she begins to trust Rover
- **[The Round Table — The Lucid Dream Decision]** At an unspecified point — either during or shortly after the main story events — Cantarella conducts the internal round table consultation described in the five character stories; she hears every voice, records every answer fairly, and chooses reality; she resolves to continue wearing the crown of thorns (the poison is inseparable), to continue brewing potions (they are needed by all in struggle), to step outside and follow the birds, and to hold onto what is real in the moment between dream and reality; the ordinary tea tastes like a breeze in the countryside
- **[Current Status — V2.8]** Cantarella is playable from Version 2.2; she serves as a Sub-DPS/healer/support in multiple team compositions; she is present in Ragunna, still head of the Fisalia family, the nightmares ceased, the restless Tacet Mark in her mouth soothed by ordinary tea, the Potion Vial imbued with trust rather than protection
