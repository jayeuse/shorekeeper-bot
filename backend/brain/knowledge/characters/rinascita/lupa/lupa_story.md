## Lupa Official Introduction

*The star gladiator of Septimont, a warrior with a pure heart, untamed spirit, intense passion, and immense potential. She will relentlessly pursue the things she admires, cherishing each moment that allows her to indulge in competition and self-expression on stage.*

A Gladiator of Septimont, a radiant star of the arena. Fiery and straightforward, Lupa lives like a wild lone wolf. As long as she can savor the adrenaline rush of battle, she doesn't mind if that same fire ends up consuming her whole.

## Lupa Personality

Lupa is uncomplicated in the best possible sense: she wants to fight, she wants those fights to be real, and she is uninterested in anything that compromises either of those requirements. The first time she stepped into Colosseum Olymdos and fought before a full crowd, she found herself laughing out loud alone in the waiting room with a bouquet of Bloodleaf Viburnum still in hand — not from performance, not from relief, but from the pure animal joy of having found the exact right place for herself to exist.

This clarity of purpose does not make her simple. She is perceptive enough to evaluate the Rover's fighting style within their first encounter and describe it as beautiful. She is loyal enough to her values that she reported her own patron House for match-fixing without visible hesitation. She is warm enough to pull the Rover out of crowds by the hand and block reporters so they can rest. She reads situations quickly and cares about the right things.

The wolf motif that permeates her identity is precise: she is not a pack creature by default. She is a lone wolf who, when she chooses someone as her partner, means it completely and immediately. The Rover becomes "my fellow warrior" within minutes of their first fight. That is not carelessness with her regard — it is the absence of any gap between what she feels and what she says.

Her arc's most harrowing element is the memory loss: a wolf who cannot remember her own territory. The Tidal Blight pursuing her through the Dark Tide arc erodes her identity progressively — she becomes "The Nameless," a being whose legs are leaden and whose thoughts are fraying, who has to pause a long while before responding, who looks at Septimont drowning and cannot tell if it can be saved. She kept moving anyway. That is the irreducible core.

## Lupa Character Story I: "Burning Inside"

*Scorching. That was Lupa's earliest memory of the small village where she was born.*

The summers of her childhood village stretched endlessly, the heat so intense that even the lake seemed to boil. She could not have known it was preparation. The children of the village barely noticed — they staged mock Agon battles in celebration of the Great Agon underway in the distant city on Capitoline Hill, picking up whatever counted as a weapon and fighting in the dust with the kind of spontaneous seriousness that children bring to contests they do not yet understand have stakes.

Lupa was called home halfway through one of those children's Agons. Her parents asked: would she be willing to move to Capitoline Hill? She said yes. Would she be willing to become a Gladiator, since she showed signs of being a Resonator? Yes. She said yes. There was no drama in either answer. This was simply what she wanted, clearly, before she had the language for what she wanted.

They arrived in Septimont: white stone walls, the crisp chime of windbells, sculptures of heroes at every turn. The city was a dazzling spectacle. But what held Lupa's attention was the most common sight in the city — the Gladiators. Their weapons, their Echoes, the way their eyes lit up as they talked about teams and scores. Every detail sent a spark through her veins. She wanted to run straight to Colosseum Olymdos.

Instead, her parents led her to the Silva estate. Many other young hopefuls stood waiting. Match after match, Lupa fought — teeth gritted, spear clutched tight, stepping over fallen rivals one by one. Until she stepped into House Silva's grand hall last. **That was when Domina Julia singled her out from the crowd.**

## Lupa Character Story II: "The Medal"

*Ever since Lupa's parents severed ties with her, their faces began to blur from her memory.*

What lingers is not their faces. What lingers is the way they used to reminisce about their days on Capitoline Hill — long, soft-voiced recollections of the city they remembered from a time before the shame. They had once borne the name Silva. They were cast out when the family they fought for lost in the arena, their lives afterward marked by regret, hardship, and the particular bitterness of blame redirected endlessly at those nearby.

When they severed ties with Lupa, they did not explain themselves. She does not record what they said. She records only that their faces began to blur afterward — a forgetting she notices but does not name as loss, or names as loss only in the noticing.

What they did after the severance is the thing the story keeps: when they heard she had won her first match, they went to a blacksmith and had a medal forged. A gift to commemorate her victory. They sent it without reconciliation, without apology, without a letter she mentions. They had the medal made.

She kept it.

The medal is the whole of the relationship now — not what they said or did not say, but this single action performed after they had already walked away. She carries it as the record of something that happened, which is different from carrying it as something that resolved.

## Lupa Character Story III: "The Nameless"

*From the shelves, she pulled out one particular recording she had watched many times before.*

Before the events of Chapter II, there was a Gladiator in Septimont's records who did not last long — who appeared suddenly, fought brilliantly, and then disappeared from the arena all too soon. Lupa found the recording and watched it repeatedly. She describes it honestly in her own narration: *perhaps a kind of self-consolation*, clinging to the idea that even someone without exceptional talent could still find joy in the arena.

The Gladiator in that recording was **Mya**. She came from Ragunna, removed the gem from her jewelry to reforge it into a helmet so no one would recognize her faith, and fought in Septimont as a gladiator who faced great hardship. The Threnodian Leviathan had been trying to take over her mind. In the end, she stabbed herself with her own sword and leapt into the sea to resist it. Her body was recovered by Julia Silva, handed to the Fractsidus, and experimented on until the Threnodian's remnant essence reawakened her corpse as the **Lady of the Sea**.

Lupa did not know any of this when she watched the recording. She watched it because the Gladiator in it fought with joy even when the situation was bad, and that was the thing Lupa needed to see.

The parallel is precise. In Chapter II Act VI — the arc in which Lupa is progressively losing her memory under the Tidal Blight, becoming "The Nameless" — she is staggering forward through a Septimont drowning in Dark Tide, struggling to form complete thoughts, pausing long before responding when Cartethyia speaks to her. Her legs are leaden. Her memories are fraying. She does not know if Septimont can still be saved.

She keeps moving.

At the conclusion of Act VI, it is Lupa who faces the Lady of the Sea in the arena's final fight. After a relentless battle, she defeats her — and **Mya's Reverberation is finally freed from its curse**. The gladiator she had watched on a screen for something like hope, whose recording she had returned to in private as evidence that the arena could hold something worth having — Lupa is the one who ends that story. She gives Mya the release that Mya could not give herself.

## Lupa: Cherished Items

**Victory Medal**
A medal forged by a blacksmith at her estranged parents' request, sent to commemorate her first arena victory. Her parents had once held the Silva name and lost it; their lives after the expulsion were marked by hardship and regret. They severed ties with Lupa and still had the medal made when they heard she had won. She keeps it. Their faces have blurred from her memory. The medal has not. It is the clearest record of something she received from them: not reconciliation, but acknowledgment — expressed sideways, through metal, after the relationship was formally over.

**Bloodleaf Viburnum (First Bouquet)**
One of many bouquets Lupa received in the arena — Bloodleaf Viburnum, the flower of Gladiators, a mark of victory and glory, and also Septimont's most iconic gladiatorial symbol. The first time she stepped into Colosseum Olymdos, she fought like a creature unleashed: relentless, instinctive, wolf-like. The crowd gathered and roared. Applause crashed over her like waves, the lights above sharp and dizzying. Only when she returned to the waiting room, the bouquet still in hand, did she hear herself laughing out loud. That first laugh — not performed, not directed at anyone, just involuntary and real — is the reference point for everything she has sought in the arena since.

## Lupa: Favorite/Disliked Food

**Favorite:** High-energy foods that match the intensity and warmth she brings to everything else — spiced, hot, straightforward. Her character design foregrounds fire in every element of her presentation, and her food preferences carry the same register: she wants things that taste like what they are, at full volume.

**Disliked:** Overly delicate or precious preparations that prioritize presentation over function. She is not interested in a meal that requires an explanation.

## Lupa: Ideals

**Passion — The Adrenaline Is the Point**
Her signature phrase makes this explicit: as long as she can savor the adrenaline rush of battle, she does not mind if the same fire ends up consuming her whole. This is not nihilism. It is the sincere expression of someone whose highest value is being fully alive in the moment she most chooses — and the arena, under full lights, against a real opponent, is that moment. She does not evaluate her life in terms of longevity or legacy; she evaluates it in terms of fire.

**Resonance — Honorable Competition**
She reported her own patron House for tampering with her spear and fixing matches. The practical consequence — losing her House affiliation, the economic structure of her competitive career, the social capital of a noble House's name behind her — was immediate and real. She did it anyway. Rigged matches are not matches. There is nothing complex about her position; it is simply total.

**Gratitude — The Recording**
She watched Mya's recordings not knowing who Mya was or what had happened to her, and found in them evidence that the arena could hold joy even for someone without exceptional gifts. That evidence mattered enough that she returned to the recording repeatedly. At the end of Act VI, she is the one who frees Mya's Reverberation from the Lady of the Sea. There is something in the structure of this — that the person who received hope from a recording became the one who closed the arc of the person in it — that functions as the game's quietest expression of what the arena is actually for.

**Acceptance — The Nameless Still Moves**
When she is losing her memory under the Tidal Blight and has been reduced to "The Nameless" — when Septimont is drowning and she cannot form a complete thought — she does not stop. The ideal she embodies here is not hope, exactly. It is the physical continuity of motion in the absence of any cognitive scaffolding for why. She walks because stopping would be the first decision she makes that she cannot walk back.

## Lupa: Narrative Chronicle

- **Early childhood:** Born in a small village near Capitoline Hill, Rinascita. The village rarely produced competitive Gladiators but celebrated the Great Agon enthusiastically; Lupa spent her childhood in mock arena battles with the other village children.
- **Move to Septimont:** Parents (estranged former House Silva members, expelled after arena defeats) move the family to Capitoline Hill. Lupa arrives in Septimont, sees the Gladiators, and immediately wants nothing else. Her parents lead her to the Silva estate rather than Colosseum Olymdos.
- **Silva selection trials:** Fights through a field of young prospects at the Silva estate in trial matches — teeth gritted, spear in hand, last one standing. Domina Julia Silva selects her from the crowd.
- **Rise through the Agons:** Fights under the Silva banner in Colosseum Olymdos. Receives her first bouquet of Bloodleaf Viburnum; laughs alone in the waiting room. Begins an undefeated record. Watches a Gladiator recording (later identified as Mya) repeatedly for consolation and inspiration.
- **Estrangement from parents:** Parents sever ties with Lupa; their faces begin to blur from her memory. They have a victory medal forged by a blacksmith for her first win and send it despite the estrangement.
- **Discovery of match-fixing:** Lupa discovers that House Silva has been tampering with matches — including her own spear. She reports them publicly, accepting the cost of losing her House affiliation. This triggers the political fallout that exposes Julia Silva in Chapter II.
- **Chapter II Act V — Shadow of Glory:** The Rover arrives in Septimont following Cartethyia's disappearance. Lupa and the Rover meet when Rover arrives in the city and crosses blades with her. They form a temporary team to enter the Great Agon together. The arc reveals corruption within Septimont's noble Houses (centered on the Silvas) and Lupa's role in exposing it. Augusta presents Julia Silva's evidence; House Silva is expelled. Lupa continues as a free gladiator.
- **Chapter II Act VI — Flames of Heart:** The Dark Tide and Tidal Blight arc escalates; Lupa begins losing her memories progressively under the Blight's influence, becoming "The Nameless." She continues operating alongside Cartethyia despite her deteriorating cognitive state. She faces and defeats the Lady of the Sea — the reanimated corpse of Mya possessed by the Threnodian — in a final arena battle. Mya's Reverberation is freed from its curse. The bouquet of Bloodleaf Viburnum in the arc's closing sequence is a direct callback to Lupa's own first bouquet and first laugh.
- **Post-arc:** Lupa and the Rover are blessed by Arsinosa; Lupa becomes a fully free gladiator, no longer affiliated with any House. The Silva family is expelled from Septimont. Julia Silva later resurfaces when the Dark Tide destroys her home and intends to fight the Undying Monster (Medulius) to reclaim her House's honor.
- **Version 2.4 Phase 2 — Playable Release (July 3, 2025):** Lupa becomes a playable character. Her kit (Wolflame, Wolfaith, Pack Hunt, Fire-Kissed Glory) encodes her gladiatorial identity: resources built through relentless combat, a team buff suite that rallies allies under her banner, and a final Dance With the Wolf that is simultaneously her most personal and most explosive moment of self-expression on the field.
